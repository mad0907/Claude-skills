# Stakeholder Map

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** a clear map of who can make or break the initiative, what they care about, where they stand today, and a specific plan to move the important ones, owned by named people and reviewed as things change.

## The senior standard
1. **Influence ≠ seniority.** Informal influencers (respected experts, union leaders, long-tenured managers, the CEO's chief of staff) often shape outcomes more than titles.
2. **Interests explain positions.** Understand what each person gains or loses (remit, budget, status, workload, job security, bonus) before trying to persuade them.
3. **Stances are dynamic.** People move as information, politics and personal impact change. Map current and target stance, and review regularly.
4. **Coalitions win.** Identify sponsors and champions early and give them roles. Plan how to convert, neutralise or work around blockers ethically.
5. **The messenger matters as much as the message.** Choose who engages whom, based on relationships and credibility.
6. **Respect and transparency.** Engagement means listening and adapting the plan, not just managing perceptions.

## Before you start: diagnose
- The initiative, its decision points and timeline.
- Who is affected, positively and negatively (internal and external)?
- Formal governance (approvers, committees) and informal networks.
- History: past initiatives and how people reacted.

## Method
1. **Identify stakeholders** broadly:
   - decision-makers;
   - approvers (boards, committees, regulators);
   - influencers;
   - implementers;
   - affected groups (employees, unions, customers, suppliers, communities);
   - external parties (investors, media).
2. **Assess each:**
   - **influence** (H/M/L; formal and informal);
   - **interest or impact** (H/M/L);
   - **current stance:** champion / supporter / neutral / sceptic / blocker;
   - **target stance;**
   - **gains and losses;**
   - **key concerns** (in their words);
   - **what they need to see.**
3. **Map relationships:** who listens to whom; trusted advisers; alliances and rivalries; influence paths to key decision-makers.
4. **Prioritise:** the high-influence stakeholders whose current stance is below target come first.
5. **Engagement plan per priority stakeholder:** objective; message framed in their interests; evidence; concessions or design changes you can offer; messenger; channel (1:1, workshop, steering role); timing; owner.
6. **Coalition design:** roles for champions (co-presenting, leading pilots); a sponsor's visible actions; how sceptics are involved (design input, pilot evaluation).
7. **Group-level plans** for large affected populations (see `change-plan`).
8. **Review rhythm:** update stances after key meetings and milestones; track the movement.

## Judgement calls
- **Sceptics often have valid points.** Incorporate their concerns into the design; converted sceptics become credible advocates.
- **Don't over-rely on the sponsor's authority.** Mandated compliance without buy-in tends to unravel after the sponsor moves on.
- **Map losers honestly.** People losing remit or jobs deserve direct, early, respectful communication and support, not spin.
- **Sensitive information:** keep stakeholder maps confidential and factual. They describe positions and interests, not personal judgements.

## Red flags
- Only senior names; no informal influencers or affected groups.
- Stances assumed rather than tested in conversation.
- One message for everyone.
- No owner per relationship.
- The map never updated after the kickoff.

## Variants by context
- **M&A integration:** both organisations' leaders, key talent, customers at risk, regulators, unions.
- **Reorganisation / GCC set-up:** affected managers, onshore teams whose work moves, HR and legal, works councils where applicable.
- **Policy / regulatory change:** regulators, industry bodies, consumer groups, media.
- **Technology / AI roll-out:** frontline users, middle managers (adoption gatekeepers), IT security, risk and compliance, employee representatives.
- **Board decisions:** individual directors' perspectives, committee chairs, major shareholders.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.
- If key inputs are missing, ask up to 3 short questions, or proceed with clearly stated assumptions if the user wants speed.
- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers.
- Remind users not to paste confidential or personal data they are not permitted to share; suggest anonymising.
