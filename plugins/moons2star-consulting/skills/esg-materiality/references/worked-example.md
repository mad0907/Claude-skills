# Worked example (illustrative; fictional Indian auto-components exporter, 40% revenue from EU OEMs)
| Topic | IRO | Value chain | Impact score | Financial score | Material? | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Climate: GHG emissions | Negative impact: 210 ktCO2e Scope 1-2; Scope 3 dominated by purchased steel and aluminium | Own ops + upstream | 4 (scale 4, scope 4, irrem. 4) | M-term 4: EU OEMs require product carbon data and reduction pathways; CBAM costs may be passed through on steel inputs (verify scope) | Both | Customer contracts at risk without a credible pathway |
| Energy | Opportunity: rooftop and open-access renewables cut Scope 2 and cost | Own ops | 3 | S-term 3: ~₹18 Cr/yr saving potential (est.) | Both | Cost and emissions co-benefit |
| Supplier labour practices | Potential negative impact: forced overtime and unsafe conditions at tier-2 forging units | Upstream | Severity 4, likelihood 3 → material (human rights) | 2 | Impact | Severity precedence; customer audits rising |
| Water | Negative impact: withdrawal in a water-stressed district (Plant C) | Own ops | 3 | 2 | Impact (borderline) | Monitor; set an intensity target |
| Product quality and safety | Risk: recalls from defective parts | Downstream | 4 | 4 | Both | Already managed under IATF; link to disclosure |
| Data protection and cyber | Risk: plant OT ransomware halts deliveries | Own ops | 2 | 4 | Financial | Just-in-time customers impose penalties |

**Most significant:** GHG (with a target and capex plan), supplier labour (due-diligence programme), product quality, cyber/OT.
