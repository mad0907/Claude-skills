# Executive Email

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** messages a busy leader can act on from the preview pane: the point and the ask first, nothing they have to dig for, and nothing you'd regret if it were forwarded.

## The senior standard
1. **Purpose and action first.** Before writing, know the one thing the reader should do or know. If it's "nothing", reconsider sending.
2. **The subject line is the summary:** "Decision needed by Thu: approve ₹2.4 Cr for pilot extension", not "Update".
3. **Bottom line up front.** The first two lines carry the ask or conclusion and the deadline.
4. **Short and scannable.** 3-5 short paragraphs or bullets; key numbers in bold sparingly; under ~200 words for most messages.
5. **Decisions come with options and a recommendation,** plus the consequence of not deciding.
6. **Explicit next steps:** who does what by when.
7. **Assume it will be forwarded.** Tone, facts and sensitive information should survive being read by anyone.

## Before you start: diagnose
- The reader(s): role, what they care about, how they read (mobile? between meetings?), relationship.
- The purpose: inform, request a decision, escalate, persuade, deliver bad news, thank, introduce.
- Deadline and urgency; who else needs to be copied.
- Sensitivity: confidential, legal, personnel, price-sensitive, regulatory.

## Method
1. **Purpose and action** in one sentence.
2. **Subject:** [Action / FYI] + topic + deadline.
3. **Opening (BLUF):** the ask or conclusion + why it matters + the deadline.
4. **Supporting points (2-4):** context, key evidence and numbers, risks.
5. **For decisions:** options (2-3) with a one-line trade-off each; the recommendation; the consequence of a delay.
6. **Next steps:** owners and dates; what you'll do after their reply.
7. **Tone:** direct, respectful, calm. No hedging piles ("just", "I think maybe"); no blame; apologise once if warranted.
8. **Sensitive content:** move it to a call where appropriate; mark "privileged and confidential" only when legal counsel is involved and the label is warranted; avoid speculation in writing.
9. **Edit:** cut 30%; one idea per paragraph; check numbers, names and dates; check attachments are referenced; the mobile preview test.
10. **Recipients:** To (action) vs Cc (inform); avoid reply-all storms; Bcc only for legitimate privacy reasons.

## Judgement calls
- **Bad news:** say it plainly in the first line, with impact, cause (brief), the fix and the ask; don't bury it.
- **Escalations:** factual, non-accusatory; what you've tried; the decision or help needed; the deadline.
- **Persuasion:** lead with the reader's interest, not yours.
- **Email vs call:** complex, emotional or contentious matters often need a call first, with email as the confirmation.
- **Cultural context:** some audiences expect a brief courtesy opening; keep it to one line.

## Red flags
- Vague subject lines.
- The ask in paragraph four.
- Walls of text; multiple unrelated topics.
- Decisions requested without options or deadlines.
- Emotional tone; speculative statements about people or legal matters.
- Wrong recipients.

## Variants by context
- **Decision request:** an options table (2-3 rows) and a recommendation.
- **Board or senior update:** 3 headlines, red / amber / green, decisions needed, risks.
- **Meeting follow-up:** decisions made, actions with owners and dates, open issues.
- **Introduction:** why the two should meet, what each gains, a suggested next step.
- **Saying no:** appreciation, a clear no, the reason (brief), an alternative if possible.
- **Messaging apps or short notes:** two lines: the point and the ask.

## Output format

```markdown
**To:** ... **Cc:** ...
**Subject:** [Action / FYI]: [topic], [deadline]

[BLUF: ask / conclusion + why + deadline]

[Supporting points, 2-4 short bullets or lines, with key numbers]

[If decision: Options: A ... / B ... → Recommend A because ... If no decision by [date]: ...]

**Next steps:** [owner, action, date]

[Sign-off]
```

- [ ] Tone safe to forward; sensitive content handled; recipients right.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.
- If key inputs are missing, ask up to 3 short questions, or proceed with clearly stated assumptions if the user wants speed.
- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers.
- Remind users not to paste confidential or personal data they are not permitted to share; suggest anonymising.
