# Worked example (illustrative; fictional consumer-durables company, FY27 growth levers)

**Original list:**
1. New customers
2. Digital
3. Pricing
4. Upselling
5. South-East Asia expansion
6. Partnerships
7. Premium range

**The whole:** all sources of incremental revenue in FY27 vs FY26.
**Purpose:** assign growth workstreams with targets.
**Verdict:** Needs work. Mixed logic (customer type, channel, price, geography, route, product), with overlaps.

| Issue | Type | Example | Size / impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Digital" overlaps "New customers" and "Upselling" | Overlap | An online first-time buyer counts in both | Double-counted targets |
| "Partnerships" is a route, not a source | Mixed logic | A retail partner could drive new customers in SE Asia | — |
| "Premium range" overlaps "Upselling" and "Pricing" | Overlap | Existing customer trades up to premium | — |
| Retention of existing customers missing | Gap | Replacement purchases by lapsed customers (~25% of revenue) | Large |
| Price/mix vs volume not separated | Mixed logic | — | Targets not additive |

**Proposed structure (logic: revenue = customers × purchases per customer × revenue per purchase, by market):**
1. **Win new customers** (rule: first purchase in the last 24 months), in India and new markets.
2. **Retain and reactivate** (rule: purchased 2-5 years ago; replacement cycle).
3. **Increase revenue per purchase:**
   3a. Price.
   3b. Mix / premium.
   3c. Attach (accessories, extended warranties).

**Markets as a second dimension:** India (existing), SE Asia (new). Targets are set in a lever × market grid.
**Cross-cutting enablers (lenses, not buckets):** digital channels, partnerships, the AI-driven personalisation engine.

**Mapping:**
- New customers → 1.
- Digital → enabler (applies to 1, 2, 3c).
- Pricing → 3a.
- Upselling → 3b/3c.
- SE Asia → market dimension.
- Partnerships → enabler.
- Premium range → 3b.

**Re-test:** 20 sample transactions each land in exactly one lever × market cell. The lever totals add up to the revenue bridge.
