# Speaker Notes

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** a presenter who sounds natural, stays on time, handles the hardest question calmly, and leaves the room with a decision, without reading slides aloud.

## The senior standard
1. **Senior meetings are discussions, not presentations.** Plan for 30-50% of the time in discussion; prepare to jump to any slide.
2. **Say the title, then the why.** Each slide opens with its message in spoken words, then the evidence. Never read bullets.
3. **Transitions carry the logic.** "So if that's the problem, what are the options?" keeps the audience in the storyline.
4. **Prepare for the worst question,** not the easy ones. Answer first, then evidence, then offer the backup.
5. **Time discipline.** Know which slides to skip if time is cut by half.
6. **Close with the ask.** Restate the decision, and confirm what happens next before people leave.

## Before you start: diagnose
- Audience (see `audience-brief`), time slot, format (in person, virtual, hybrid), and whether a pre-read was sent.
- The deck and its storyline; your role (owner or presenting for others).
- Known sensitivities and the likely hardest questions.

## Method
1. **Time plan:** allocate minutes per slide or section. Key slides get more time, context slides less. Reserve discussion time. If a pre-read was sent, plan to summarise in 5 minutes and spend the rest discussing.
2. **Opening (≤60 seconds):** purpose, the ask, and why now ("We're asking you to approve X today because Y").
3. **Per slide:**
   - an **opening line** (the title in natural speech);
   - **2-3 talking points** (evidence, example, implication);
   - a **transition;**
   - **cues** (pause for questions; emphasise a number).
4. **Skip markers:** which slides to drop if time is cut, and the one-sentence bridge if you skip them.
5. **Hard-question bank (8-12):** answer-first responses (≤30 seconds), the supporting fact, and a backup slide reference. Include "What if you're wrong?", "Why not option B?", "Who's accountable?", "What does it cost if it slips?"
6. **Difficult moments:**
   - **Hostile challenge:** acknowledge, answer the substance, offer a follow-up.
   - **Off-track discussion:** park it with an owner.
   - **"I don't know":** commit to a follow-up by a date.
   - **Time cut:** go to the summary and the ask.
7. **Close (≤60 seconds):** restate the ask; summarise the conditions; confirm next steps and owners.
8. **Virtual or hybrid:** shorter segments; call on remote participants by name; have a co-host manage chat; share the deck link as a backup.
9. **Rehearsal plan:** one full run-through out loud; a Q&A drill with a colleague playing the toughest audience member.

## Judgement calls
- **Pre-read meetings:** don't re-present. Ask, "What questions do you have?" after a 3-minute summary.
- **If the decision-maker signals early agreement,** stop presenting, confirm the decision and conditions, and move on.
- **Numbers to memorise:** the 5-7 key figures. Looking them up mid-answer erodes credibility.
- **Co-presenters:** agree handovers and who answers which topics.

## Red flags
- Notes that duplicate slide text.
- No discussion time planned.
- Only easy questions prepared.
- No closing ask; the meeting ends with "any questions?"
- Running over time with the key slides still unshown.

## Variants by context
- **Board:** the Chair controls the agenda; address the Chair; be concise; don't argue with directors, but clarify and offer follow-ups.
- **Investors:** a consistent narrative; careful forward-looking statements; no new price-sensitive information beyond what's disclosed.
- **Client pitch:** client-centred opening; questions to engage; tailored examples.
- **Town hall:** plain language, empathy, honest answers about impact; a Q&A moderator.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.
- If key inputs are missing, ask up to 3 short questions, or proceed with clearly stated assumptions if the user wants speed.
- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers.
- Remind users not to paste confidential or personal data they are not permitted to share; suggest anonymising.
