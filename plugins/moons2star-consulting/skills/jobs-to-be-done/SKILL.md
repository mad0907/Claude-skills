# Jobs To Be Done

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** a clear understanding of the progress customers are trying to make, where today's solutions fail them, and what would make them switch, turned into opportunities your teams can build and sell.

## The senior standard
1. **Jobs are stable; solutions change.** Define the job independently of any product ("get paid on time by customers", not "use invoicing software").
2. **Functional, emotional and social.** Executive buyers hire solutions to look competent, reduce personal risk and gain control, not just to complete tasks.
3. **Outcomes are measurable.** Phrase desired outcomes as direction + metric + object + context ("minimise the time to reconcile bank statements at month-end").
4. **Find underserved outcomes.** Opportunity = high importance with low satisfaction. Overserved outcomes indicate room to simplify or reduce price.
5. **Switching has forces.** The push of the current situation and the pull of the new solution must overcome the anxiety of the new and the habit of the present.
6. **Evidence from real behaviour.** Interview recent switchers about the actual purchase story (timeline, triggers, alternatives considered), not hypothetical preferences.

## Before you start: diagnose
- The customer type (job executor, buyer, decision-makers) and the market.
- Existing research (interviews, reviews, support tickets, win/loss).
- The decision (product roadmap, positioning, a new venture).

## Method
1. **Define the job executor** and the **core functional job** (verb + object + context).
2. **Related jobs** (adjacent tasks done alongside); **emotional jobs** (how they want to feel or avoid feeling); **social jobs** (how they want to be perceived).
3. **Job map:** for each step (define, locate, prepare, confirm, execute, monitor, modify, conclude), describe what the executor is trying to accomplish.
4. **Desired outcomes:** 3-8 per key step, as measurable statements.
5. **Score outcomes:** importance and satisfaction (from a survey, or estimated from interviews). Opportunity score = importance + max(importance − satisfaction, 0); state the scale used.
6. **Switching forces:** from switch interviews, capture push (problems with the current situation), pull (attraction of the new), anxiety (fears about the new), and habit (inertia), plus the triggering events.
7. **Opportunities:** underserved outcomes → product features, services, business models; overserved → simplify or a lower-cost offer; switching insights → messaging, onboarding, risk reversal (trials, guarantees).
8. **Validate:** test concepts or messages against the outcomes with real customers.

## Judgement calls
- **The buyer isn't always the executor.** In B2B, map the jobs of users, buyers, approvers and IT separately.
- **Don't over-engineer surveys.** Even qualitative ranking from 15-20 good interviews can reveal the main opportunities.
- **Watch for non-consumption:** people who don't use any solution because existing ones are too costly or complex. This is often the biggest opportunity.
- **AI changes job maps.** Steps like "locate" and "prepare" may be automated. Customers still need to "confirm" and "monitor", so design for trust.

## Red flags
- Jobs that name a product or feature.
- Outcomes that are vague ("better insights").
- Emotional and social jobs ignored for executive buyers.
- Evidence from hypothetical questions only.
- Opportunities not linked to specific outcomes.

## Variants by context
- **B2B software:** job maps for users vs buyers; risk-reduction and compliance outcomes.
- **Financial services:** life-event triggers (new business, home purchase); anxiety reduction is critical.
- **Healthcare:** patient, caregiver and clinician jobs; adherence and reassurance.
- **Consumer goods:** situational jobs (time of day, occasion), social signalling.
- **Internal services (IT, HR, finance):** jobs of internal customers; service design.

## House rules

- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
