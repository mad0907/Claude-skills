# Worked example (illustrative; fictional company)

**Paper:** Management seeks approval for ₹500 crore over 3 years for an enterprise GenAI platform (internal copilots, customer-service agents, document processing) at a mid-size Indian private-sector insurer.

**Critical assumptions identified:**
1. Productivity gains of 25-30% in claims and servicing translate into cost savings, not just "time freed".
2. Customer-facing agents can operate within IRDAI and DPDP obligations without material conduct risk.
3. The organisation can absorb the change (adoption ≥70% within 12 months).

## Top questions
| # | Question | Why it matters | A good answer includes | Red flags | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Of the ₹500 Cr, how much is committed vs released only when value is shown, and what are the stage gates? | Limits irreversible commitment | Tranches tied to measured outcomes; who decides at each gate | The full amount committed upfront; vendor multi-year minimums | "What do we lose if we stop after year 1?" |
| 2 | How much of the claimed saving is cash we will actually remove from the cost base, and who signs up to it in their budget? | Most AI cases overstate cash benefits | Benefits split into hard and soft; budget owners named; phasing | "Capacity released" with no redeployment plan | "Which budgets go down next year, and by how much?" |
| 3 | Which AI uses will make or influence decisions about customers (claims, pricing, servicing), and how are they classified and controlled? | Conduct, fairness and regulatory exposure | Inventory; risk tiers; human review for adverse decisions; testing across customer groups | No inventory yet; "the model only assists" with no evidence | "Show us last month's override and complaint data from the pilot." |
| 4 | Where does policyholder data go, and under which DPDP obligations and timelines do we operate? | Legal exposure; trust | Data flows mapped; processing location; vendor terms on training use; DPDP readiness plan by the May 2027 obligations | "The vendor is compliant" | "Has counsel reviewed the vendor contract's data clauses?" |
| 5 | What can the customer-service agent do without a human, and how do we stop it if it misbehaves? | Agentic risk; kill switch | Defined autonomy limits; escalation rules; kill switch tested; logging | Broad tool access; no tested shutdown | "When was the kill switch last tested?" |
| 6 | Who is personally accountable for the programme and for AI risk, and are they different people? | Separation of delivery and oversight | Named business owner; independent second-line AI risk oversight | Same person builds and approves | "What does the CRO's team review before go-live?" |
| 7 | What did the pilots show on accuracy, hallucination and customer outcomes, and which pilot did we stop? | Evidence over enthusiasm | Measured results against a baseline; at least one stopped or changed | Every pilot "successful" | "What was the error rate on the hardest 10% of cases?" |
| 8 | How dependent will we be on one model provider, and what is the exit plan? | Concentration and lock-in | Model-agnostic architecture; exit and data-return terms | Single proprietary stack, no exit clause | "What would switching cost us in year 2?" |
| 9 | How will employees be affected, and what is the reskilling and communication plan? | Culture, adoption, reputation | Role-impact analysis; reskilling budget; union or works-council engagement where relevant | "No job losses" asserted with no plan | "What are we telling staff, and when?" |

## Ask for
- Before approval: an AI inventory and risk-tiering of the planned use cases; the second-line view; counsel's note on DPDP and IRDAI obligations.
- Ongoing (quarterly): value realised vs plan (hard savings); adoption; incidents, complaints and overrides; model performance by customer segment; spend vs tranche.
- Annually: independent review (internal audit or external) of AI governance against an accepted framework (e.g. ISO/IEC 42001 or NIST AI RMF).

## Note to Chair / CEO before the meeting
"Ahead of item 6, it would help if management could bring: (1) the split of committed vs gated spend; (2) the pilot results, including error rates and the override and complaint data; (3) a one-page view from the CRO on AI risk tiering and controls; and (4) counsel's summary of DPDP obligations and timelines. Happy to discuss beforehand."
