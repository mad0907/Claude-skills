# Board Question Bank: topic packs

Each pack: core questions → what good looks like → red flags. Pick, adapt and rank; don't read them all out.

## 1. Strategy and annual plan
- What are the two or three choices in this strategy that we are deliberately *not* making, and why?
- Which assumptions about customers, competitors, technology or regulation would most change this plan, and what are we watching to detect a shift?
- How does the capital allocation (Run / Grow / Transform, or by business) reflect the strategy? Where are we under-investing?
- What did we commit to last year, and what did we deliver? What explains the gap?
- What is the plan if the market grows at half the assumed rate?

**Good looks like:** explicit trade-offs, leading indicators, a variance bridge to the prior plan, a downside case with management actions.
**Red flags:** a plan that is last year +10%; no downside case; strategy and budget owned by different people and not reconciled.

## 2. M&A / strategic investment
- Why is owning this better than partnering or building, and why are we the best owner?
- What premium are we paying, and which specific synergies justify it? Who owns each synergy, by when, and at what cost to achieve?
- What did diligence find that we didn't expect, and how is it reflected in price, structure or conditions?
- What are the walk-away price and conditions, and who has authority to walk?
- How will integration be led, and what are the first 100 days? What is the cultural risk?
- What happened with our last two acquisitions against their cases?

**Good looks like:** synergies phased and owned; price discipline (a walk-away agreed before negotiation); a post-deal review date set now.
**Red flags:** "strategic" used to justify any price; synergies mostly revenue; integration planning starts after signing; deal-team fatigue or a sunk-cost tone.

## 3. Capex and major projects
- What is the estimate class and its accuracy range? What contingency is included, and on what basis?
- What is the critical path, and what is the single biggest schedule risk?
- What are the stage gates and kill criteria? Who decides at each gate?
- What did our last project of this size overrun by, and what's different now?

**Red flags:** contingency set as a round number with no basis; no independent cost review on large projects; benefits depend on a later phase not yet approved.

## 4. Risk and internal control
- What are our top risks today, and how has that list changed in the last 12 months?
- Where are we operating outside appetite, and what is the plan and timeline to return?
- Which risks are emerging but not yet on the register?
- How do we know controls work, beyond management saying so? What do internal audit and other independent assurance say?
- What near-misses or incidents happened this quarter, and what did we learn?

**Red flags:** an unchanged risk register; all risks amber; no near-miss reporting (which means a non-reporting culture, not a safe one).

## 5. Audit Committee: reporting, auditors and IFC
- What were the significant judgements and estimates this period, and how sensitive are results to them?
- Where did management and the auditors disagree, and how was it resolved?
- Any changes in accounting policies or presentation? Why?
- What did internal audit find, and how many past actions are overdue?
- What is the status of internal financial controls (s.134(5)(e))? Any deficiencies, and their root causes?
- Has the auditor's independence been tested, including non-audit services?
- What do whistle-blower reports show about themes, closure times and retaliation?
- What does the auditor want to tell us without management in the room?

**Red flags:** no auditor-only session; overdue audit actions accepted repeatedly; aggressive estimates that are always favourable.

## 6. Cyber and operational resilience
- What are our critical business services, and what are the impact tolerances (maximum tolerable disruption)?
- When did we last test recovery of a critical system end to end, and what failed?
- How quickly can we detect, contain and report an incident? (Note mandatory reporting windows, such as CERT-In directions in India.)
- Which third parties could take us down, and what is our concentration risk?
- What would a ransomware attack cost us in a week, and how is it insured or mitigated?

**Red flags:** no tested restores; "we have a firewall" answers; cyber reported only as a list of projects.

## 7. AI and data (including GenAI and agents)
- Where are we using AI today, including inside vendor products and staff use of public tools? Do we have a complete inventory?
- Which AI uses affect customers', employees' or citizens' rights, money, health or access to services? How are they classified and controlled?
- Who is accountable for each high-impact AI system, and who can stop it?
- How do we test for accuracy, bias and robustness before launch and in production? What were the results?
- What data trains or feeds these systems, and do we have the right to use it (consent, purpose, IP)?
- For GenAI and agents: what stops harmful or false output, prompt injection, or data leakage? What can an agent do without human approval?
- What incidents or complaints have there been, and how were they handled?
- Which laws apply to us (e.g. DPDP in India, the EU AI Act if we serve the EU, sector rules) and what are the dates?
- How do we measure value from AI, and which pilots have we stopped?

**Red flags:** no inventory; "the vendor handles that"; accuracy reported only on average; no kill switch; GenAI policy without monitoring; every pilot "promising".

## 8. ESG, climate and BRSR
- What are our material topics, and how were they determined (double materiality, stakeholder input)?
- What are our transition and physical climate risks, quantified where possible, and how do they enter strategy and capital allocation?
- Are our targets credible? Show the decarbonisation pathway, capex and dependencies.
- How assurance-ready are our BRSR Core data? Who owns each KPI, and what is the evidence trail?
- What value-chain data do we depend on, and how reliable is it?
- What is our exposure to export-market rules (e.g. carbon border mechanisms) or customer requirements?

**Red flags:** targets with no capex; ESG data from spreadsheets with no controls; ESG handled only by CSR or communications.

## 9. People, culture, succession and remuneration
- Who could step in tomorrow for the CEO and each critical role, and in 2-3 years?
- What do engagement, attrition (regretted), grievance and whistle-blower data tell us about culture?
- Do incentives reward the right behaviours, including risk and compliance, not just growth?
- What is the board hearing directly from employees below the leadership team?

## 10. Crisis and incidents
- What happened, what is the impact, and what is still unknown?
- Who is in charge, and what decisions are needed from the board now?
- What have we told regulators, customers, employees and the market, and when must we?
- What is the root cause, and are there other places it could happen?
- How will we look after the people affected?

## 11. Related-party transactions
- Why is this in the company's interest compared with an unrelated party?
- What is the arm's-length evidence (independent valuation, benchmarks)?
- Is it in the ordinary course of business? Who decided that, and on what basis?
- Which directors are interested, and have they abstained?

## Register by role
- **Chair:** frame the decision; ensure every director is heard; summarise conditions; agree what comes back.
- **Committee chair presenting to the board:** report the committee's view, the dissent and the conditions; don't re-run the committee debate.
- **New director:** ask clarifying, context-seeking questions ("Help me understand how we got here…"). Read prior minutes first.
- **Independent director:** prioritise minority shareholders and stakeholders, conflicts and integrity; you may need to insist on independent advice.

## Phrasing toolkit
- "Help me understand…" / "Walk us through…"
- "What would have to be true for…?"
- "What's the strongest argument against this?"
- "How will we know, early, that it isn't working?"
- "What would you do if it were your own money?"
- "If this appeared on the front page tomorrow, how would it read?"
