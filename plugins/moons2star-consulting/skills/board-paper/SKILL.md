# Board Paper

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** a paper a time-poor, legally accountable director can read in 10-15 minutes and use to take a defensible decision. That means one with an informed basis, alternatives considered, risks understood and dissent possible.

## The standard to write to

A board paper is not a management presentation. Directors owe duties of care, skill, diligence and independent judgement (Companies Act 2013, s.166). They can only meet those duties if the paper gives them:
1. **What is being asked**, in the first half page.
2. **Why it is in the company's interest**, against the strategy and risk appetite the board has already approved.
3. **What else was considered**, and why it was rejected.
4. **What could go wrong**, how likely it is and how bad, and how the board will know early.
5. **Who is conflicted**, and what law or policy applies.
6. **What exactly will be resolved**, and what is delegated to whom, within what limits.

**The test:** if this decision went badly in three years, would the minutes and this paper show the board asked the right questions on an informed basis? If not, the paper is not ready.

## Before you draft: diagnose the paper

Ask (or infer and state):
- **Purpose:** For decision, For discussion (shaping a decision coming later) or For noting. Most bad papers are unclear on this. Never label a paper "for noting" when it carries a decision in disguise, such as a new risk accepted or a commitment made.
- **Forum:** full board, or a committee (Audit, NRC, Risk Management, CSR, Stakeholders Relationship, Investment). Check that the matter falls within the forum's terms of reference and delegation of authority. Matters reserved for the board, or needing shareholder approval, must be flagged.
- **Decision class:** reversible or irreversible; size relative to net worth or turnover; whether it triggers disclosure (SEBI LODR Reg 30 materiality), related-party approval (Reg 23 / s.188), or special majorities.
- **Audience state:** what the board already knows and decided before (cite earlier resolutions), and which directors have expertise or known concerns. Plan pre-wiring with the Chair and the relevant committee chair.
- **Timing:** SS-1 expects notes on agenda to be circulated at least seven days before the meeting. Shorter notice for unpublished price-sensitive information or urgent items needs the conditions in SS-1 to be met. Late papers erode trust; say why if unavoidable.

## Structure (main paper 4-6 pages; detail in annexes)

1. **Cover block:** title; meeting and date; agenda item number; purpose (Decision / Discussion / Noting); sponsor (accountable executive); author; classification (e.g. Confidential, contains UPSI); previous board references.
2. **Executive summary (half a page, maximum):**
   - **The ask**, in one sentence with the amount, scope and timing.
   - **Why now**: the trigger, and the cost of delay.
   - **The recommendation** and the two or three reasons that carry it.
   - **The key risks** and the residual risk position after mitigation.
   - **Any conflicts**, and the approvals needed beyond this board.
3. **Strategic context:** link to the approved strategy, plan and risk appetite. State plainly if the proposal stretches or departs from them.
4. **Options considered:** at least three, including "do nothing / defer". Use a comparison table on the criteria the board cares about (value, risk, capital, time, reversibility, stakeholder impact). Explain why the rejected options were rejected. Directors will ask.
5. **Financial impact:**
   - Investment, phasing and funding source.
   - Effect on P&L, cash, balance sheet, key ratios and covenants (e.g. net debt/EBITDA headroom).
   - Returns (NPV, IRR, payback) against the hurdle rate, and the sensitivity of those returns to the two or three assumptions that matter.
   - Separate committed from contingent spend.
6. **Risks:**
   - The top five, each written as cause, event and consequence.
   - Inherent and residual ratings, mitigations with owners, and early-warning indicators.
   - Fit with risk appetite.
   - Include execution capacity (people, management bandwidth) and reputational and ESG risk, not only financial risk.
7. **Legal, regulatory, governance and stakeholder considerations:**
   - Approvals needed (regulators, shareholders, lenders, CCI for combinations).
   - Disclosure obligations.
   - Related-party aspects and interested directors (s.184 disclosure; abstention).
   - Employee, community and customer impacts.
   - Data-protection or AI-governance implications where relevant.
8. **Implementation and monitoring:** milestones, stage gates and kill criteria; what comes back to the board, when, and in what form (KPIs with thresholds).
9. **Recommendation and draft resolution:** precise legal wording, including any delegation (to whom, what limits, until when) and authority to sign or file.
10. **Annexes:** financial model summary, due diligence findings, legal opinions, detailed risk register, glossary.

See `references/worked-example.md` for a complete paper, and `references/deep-dive.md` for resolution wording patterns, checklists by decision type, and a reviewer's scoring rubric.

## Judgement calls a senior author makes

- **Lead with the uncomfortable fact.** If the IRR is close to the hurdle, or diligence found a gap, put it in the executive summary. Directors discover buried bad news, and trust does not recover.
- **One recommendation, honestly argued.** Present real alternatives. Asking the board to "choose between options" without a management view usually signals unclear ownership. The exception is a Discussion paper seeking direction.
- **Quantify uncertainty; don't decorate it.** Give ranges and the break-even value of the key assumption, e.g. "NPV turns negative if volumes are 18% below plan", instead of "robust NPV".
- **Separate facts, estimates and judgements.** Label each one: audited, management estimate, adviser view, or management judgement.
- **Make dissent easy.** Note where management or advisers disagreed and how it was resolved. Boards value seeing the debate.
- **Size the paper to the decision.** A ₹5 crore renewal does not need 40 pages. An acquisition needs a full annex set. Length is not diligence.
- **Write for the least-expert director in the room**, without patronising the most expert. Define terms once; give technical depth in annexes.
- **Think about the minutes.** The paper should make it easy for the Company Secretary to minute the basis of the decision and any directions given.

## Red flags when reviewing someone else's paper

- The executive summary has no number in it, or the ask appears only on the last page.
- Only one real option, plus straw men.
- Returns shown without sensitivity, or sensitivity only on assumptions that don't matter.
- "Risks: low" with no mechanism. Mitigations that are intentions ("we will monitor closely").
- No mention of conflicts, related parties or approvals beyond the board, when the transaction obviously involves them.
- A resolution broader than the paper, e.g. "authorised to do all acts as may be necessary" with no limits or end date.
- New numbers that do not reconcile with the last plan or budget the board approved, and no bridge explaining the difference.
- Circulated late, with "to be tabled" annexes.

## Variants by context

- **Listed company (India):** check Reg 30 disclosure timing; whether the item is UPSI (handle the trading window and structured digital database); Reg 23 for related-party transactions (audit committee approval; shareholder approval above materiality thresholds, so verify the current threshold); and Reg 17 / Schedule II board information requirements.
- **Regulated financial entity (bank, NBFC, insurer):** map to sector directions on board approvals (e.g. outsourcing, IT and cyber, credit policy, AI or model risk where applicable) and to any fit-and-proper or regulator pre-approval requirement.
- **Subsidiary of a global group:** note the parent's reserved matters and approvals, and transfer-pricing or FEMA aspects.
- **Unlisted / private / family company:** shareholder-agreement reserved matters and investor consent rights often decide more than the board does. List them.
- **Public sector:** DPE guidelines and CVC considerations on procurement; approval hierarchies.
- **Committee papers:** Audit Committee papers need the internal-control and accounting-treatment angle. NRC papers need benchmarking and succession logic. Risk Committee papers need appetite fit and stress results.

## Quality check before you reply
- [ ] The purpose label matches what is actually being asked.
- [ ] The ask, amount and timing are in the first three lines.
- [ ] At least one real alternative and "do nothing", with reasons for rejection.
- [ ] Returns shown against the hurdle, with break-even on the key assumption.
- [ ] Risks have owners, residual ratings and early-warning indicators, and fit with appetite is stated.
- [ ] Conflicts, related parties, disclosures and further approvals are addressed, or explicitly marked not applicable.
- [ ] The resolution is precise, with limits and an end date on any delegation.
- [ ] Monitoring: what returns to the board, when, and the kill criteria.
- [ ] Facts, estimates and judgements are labelled; regulatory points are flagged for Company Secretary or counsel to verify.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.
- If key inputs are missing, ask up to 3 short questions, or proceed with clearly stated assumptions if the user wants speed.
- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers (Company Secretary, legal counsel, auditors).
- Remind users not to paste confidential or price-sensitive information they are not permitted to share; suggest anonymising.
