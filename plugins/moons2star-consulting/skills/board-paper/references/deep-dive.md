# Board Paper: deep dive

## A. Checklists by decision type

### Capital expenditure / new plant / major project
- Strategic fit, and the capacity or demand case (utilisation of existing assets first).
- Capex phasing; committed vs uncommitted; contingency (state the % and its basis).
- Returns vs WACC or hurdle; payback; sensitivity to volume, price, capex overrun (+15-25% is common for greenfield), and delay.
- Funding: internal accruals, debt (covenant headroom, rating impact), equity.
- Execution: EPC model, vendor concentration, key-person risk, approvals (environmental clearance, land, utilities).
- Stage gates: e.g. release 30% now; the balance subject to milestones and a demand check.

### Acquisition / investment / JV
- Thesis: why this asset, why now, why us (synergies by type, with phasing and cost to achieve).
- Valuation: method triangulation (DCF, trading comparables, transaction comparables); premium paid vs synergies; what the board is paying for that it would not get standalone.
- Diligence: financial, legal, tax, HR, IT/cyber, ESG and data protection. Red flags and how they are handled: price adjustment, indemnity, escrow, conditions precedent.
- Structure: stake, control rights, put/call options, earn-outs, valuation of options.
- Approvals: CCI (combination thresholds or deal-value test), sector regulator, FEMA/ODI for overseas, shareholders.
- Integration plan, owner and 100-day priorities; how and when the board will review post-deal performance against the case.

### Related-party transaction
- Nature, value and terms; arm's-length evidence (benchmarks, independent valuation).
- Ordinary course of business: yes or no, and why.
- Approvals: audit committee (only independent directors vote on RPTs under LODR); board; shareholders if material; interested directors abstain.
- Disclosure requirements; omnibus approval limits if used.

### Policy approval (e.g. AI policy, risk management policy, whistle-blower, dividend)
- What the policy changes vs the current state, and why.
- Who it applies to; how compliance will be monitored and reported.
- Regulatory basis (cite) and benchmark against peers.
- Review cycle.

### Annual plan / budget
- Bridge from last year's actuals and from the last strategy plan.
- Key assumptions and their sensitivities; stretch vs base.
- Capital allocation across Run / Grow / Transform, or by business unit.
- Risks to the plan and in-year triggers for reforecast.

### Senior appointment / remuneration (NRC → Board)
- Process followed (search, diversity of slate, assessment).
- Fit with the skills matrix; independence where relevant.
- Remuneration benchmarking and policy compliance; shareholder approval needs.

## B. Resolution wording patterns (adapt with the Company Secretary)

**Approval with limits and delegation**
> RESOLVED THAT approval of the Board be and is hereby accorded for [action], at a total cost not exceeding ₹[x] crore (inclusive of contingency of ₹[y] crore), to be incurred during FY[..] to FY[..], substantially on the terms set out in the note placed before the Board.
>
> FURTHER RESOLVED THAT [Managing Director / CFO / Committee] be and is hereby authorised to [negotiate, finalise and execute definitive agreements / release funds in tranches as per the milestones in Annex X], provided that any variation exceeding [5]% of the approved cost or any material change in terms shall be placed before the Board for approval.
>
> FURTHER RESOLVED THAT [names/designations] be and are hereby severally authorised to sign and file such documents and do such acts as may be necessary to give effect to this resolution, [until (date)].

**Discussion paper outcome (no resolution; minuted direction)**
> The Board discussed [item] and directed management to [develop options A and B in detail / test assumption X / return by (meeting) with ...].

**Noting**
> The Board noted [item]. (Use only when no new decision, risk acceptance or commitment is involved.)

Drafting cautions:
- Avoid open-ended "do all acts, deeds and things" without limits.
- Name the delegate by designation, not by person.
- Tie delegation to the paper's annex, so the scope is evidenced.

## C. Reviewer's scoring rubric (score 1-5 each; below 3 on any one means rework)

| Dimension | 1 (weak) | 3 (adequate) | 5 (excellent) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clarity of ask | Ask unclear or at end | Ask clear but buried in page 2 | Ask, amount and timing in the first 3 lines |
| Options | One option | Alternatives listed, little analysis | Real alternatives compared on agreed criteria, with reasons for rejection |
| Financial rigour | Totals only | Returns vs hurdle | Returns, break-even of the key driver, covenant and ratio impact, funding |
| Risk | Generic list | Ratings and mitigations | Cause-event-consequence, residual vs appetite, early-warning indicators, owners |
| Governance and compliance | Silent | Mentions approvals | Conflicts, RPTs, disclosures, regulators and reserved matters fully addressed |
| Monitoring | None | Promises updates | Stage gates, KPIs with thresholds, kill criteria, reporting dates |
| Readability | Deck pasted as paper | Structured, but long | Main paper ≤6 pages; annexes do the heavy lifting; terms defined |
| Candour | Bad news absent | Mentioned in annex | Key weaknesses in the executive summary, with management's response |

## D. Common director questions a paper should pre-empt
1. What happens if we don't do this, or do it in a year?
2. What did you consider and reject?
3. What is the one assumption that, if wrong, sinks the case?
4. Do we have the management capacity to deliver this alongside everything else?
5. How does this change our risk profile, and is it within appetite?
6. Who is conflicted, and what approvals remain?
7. How will we know early that it is going wrong, and what will we do then?
8. How does this look to regulators, investors and employees if it becomes public tomorrow?

## E. India-specific mechanics to confirm with the Company Secretary
- Notice and circulation of agenda and notes (SS-1); shorter-notice conditions.
- Quorum and interested-director rules (s.174, s.184).
- Matters that must be decided at a meeting, not by circular resolution (s.179(3) and rules).
- Matters that need shareholder approval (e.g. s.180 borrowing and sale-of-undertaking limits; s.186 loans and investments beyond limits; s.188 / Reg 23 RPTs).
- Disclosure timelines (Reg 30 and Schedule III).
- UPSI handling and the structured digital database (SEBI PIT Regulations).
