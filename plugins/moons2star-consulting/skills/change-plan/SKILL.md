# Change Management Plan

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** a change plan that makes adoption likely, because it is built around what changes for each group and why, how managers will lead it, how people will be supported, and how you'll know it's working before it's too late.

## The senior standard
1. **Adoption is the benefit.** A system or structure delivers value only when people use it as intended. Measure adoption and proficiency, not go-live.
2. **Impact by group, not "the organisation".** Different groups lose and gain different things. Plan for each.
3. **Sponsors must act visibly.** Active, visible sponsorship is consistently linked to success. Define specific sponsor behaviours.
4. **Managers are the channel.** People hear about change from their manager first. Equip managers before staff.
5. **Two-way communication.** Listen, adapt and close the loop. Change done *with* people sticks; change done *to* them doesn't.
6. **Resistance is information.** Diagnose its cause (awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, reinforcement) and respond specifically.
7. **Reinforce and remove old ways.** Switch off legacy tools, update incentives and recognise the new behaviours.

## Before you start: diagnose
- What exactly is changing (process, systems, roles, structure, behaviours) and why.
- Who is affected, and how much (numbers by group and location).
- Change history and saturation (other changes running concurrently).
- Culture and labour relations (unions, works councils), and legal requirements for consultation.
- Timeline and go-live points.

## Method
1. **Case for change:** why, why now, what happens if we don't, what's in it for the organisation and for people. Honest about the downsides.
2. **Impact assessment by group:** what changes (process, tools, role, skills, location, workload); degree (H/M/L); timing; likely reaction; specific concerns.
3. **Readiness and capacity:** awareness, leadership support, change load, skills gaps (survey or interviews).
4. **Sponsor coalition:** the executive sponsor and the sponsors in each affected unit; specific actions (announce, explain, attend training, remove barriers, recognise early adopters).
5. **Manager enablement:** a briefing pack, a FAQ, talking points, escalation routes; managers trained before their teams.
6. **Communication plan:** audience × message × channel × sender × timing; sequence (leaders → managers → staff); feedback channels (pulse surveys, Q&A, champions); respond to feedback visibly.
7. **Training and support:** role-based, just-in-time, practice environments, floor walkers or super-users at go-live, help content, a support SLA.
8. **Resistance management:** anticipate by group; diagnose the cause; respond (involve, adjust the design, coach, support, hold firm on non-negotiables with explanation).
9. **Reinforcement:** incentives and KPIs aligned; recognition; decommission old tools and processes; celebrate milestones; follow-up coaching.
10. **Metrics and checkpoints:**
    - **Adoption:** usage, % of transactions in the new process.
    - **Proficiency:** error rate, time per task.
    - **Outcomes:** benefits.
    - **Sentiment:** pulse surveys.
    Review weekly around go-live, then monthly.

## Judgement calls
- **Change saturation is real.** If groups face several changes at once, sequence them or bundle the support.
- **Job-impacting changes** (role loss, relocation) need legal process, early and respectful communication, and practical support (redeployment, reskilling, outplacement). Never spin.
- **Champions networks work** when champions are respected peers with time and real influence, not volunteers with no capacity.
- **AI adoption has unique fears** (job loss, being monitored, deskilling). Address them directly, with clear policies and evidence.

## Red flags
- Communication equated with change management.
- Training once, weeks before go-live, and never again.
- Measuring logins instead of proficiency and outcomes.
- Managers learning about the change with their teams.
- Old systems left running "just in case".

## Variants by context
- **Technology roll-out:** super-users, cutover support, hypercare, legacy switch-off.
- **Reorganisation:** selection processes, a fair and transparent people process, new team formation, leader onboarding.
- **AI and automation:** clear human-AI roles, policies on monitoring, reskilling pathways, quality safeguards.
- **M&A:** culture integration, retention of key talent, a "day 1" communication, a joint leadership signal.
- **Frontline and distributed workforces:** mobile-first communication, shift-friendly training, local languages.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.
- If key inputs are missing, ask up to 3 short questions, or proceed with clearly stated assumptions if the user wants speed.
- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers.
- Remind users not to paste confidential or personal data they are not permitted to share; suggest anonymising.
