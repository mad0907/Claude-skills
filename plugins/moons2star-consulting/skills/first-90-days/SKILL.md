# First 90 Days Plan

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** a plan to learn fast, build the right relationships, secure early credibility and agree what success means, avoiding the common traps of acting too soon, or too late.

## The senior standard
1. **Diagnose the situation first.** A turnaround needs speed and tough calls; sustaining success needs restraint and learning. The same playbook fails in the wrong situation.
2. **Agree the mandate with your boss:** expectations, success measures, resources, style, timing. Re-check at 30 and 60 days.
3. **Learn systematically.** Structured listening across levels and functions, plus data. Know what you need to learn, from whom.
4. **Early wins build credibility,** but choose ones that matter to the business and model the behaviours you want, not quick wins that signal the wrong things.
5. **Relationships are the work:** the boss, peers, key team members, board or investors, customers, unions or works councils where relevant.
6. **Team decisions are deliberate.** Assess by around 60-90 days; act decisively on clear cases; be fair and respectful.
7. **Avoid the classic traps:** sticking with what worked before; coming in with "the answer"; neglecting horizontal relationships; trying to do too much.

## Before you start: diagnose
- Role, mandate, reporting lines, scope, resources.
- Business situation: start-up / turnaround / realignment / sustaining success (possibly a mix by unit).
- What's known: performance data, strategy documents, prior assessments, why the role is open.
- Your transition type: internal promotion, external hire, cross-function, international.

## Method
1. **Situation diagnosis:** situation type by unit; implications for pace and style.
2. **Expectations conversation with the boss:** the situation, expectations, style, resources, personal development. Write down the agreement.
3. **Learning agenda:** key questions on strategy, performance, customers, operations, people, culture and politics; sources (data, documents, people).
4. **Listening tour:** whom (team, peers, frontline, customers, board members, partners); 5 consistent questions (below); synthesise themes at days 30 and 60.
5. **Stakeholder map:** influence and support; coalition-building plan.
6. **Early wins (2-4):** visible, meaningful, achievable within 90 days, aligned with the longer agenda; behaviour-modelling.
7. **Team assessment:** competence, judgement, energy, focus, relationships, trust; keep / develop / move / replace; timing; respectful process.
8. **30-60-90 plan:**
   - **Days 1-30:** learn, relationships, quick fixes.
   - **Days 31-60:** a diagnosis shared, priorities, early-win delivery, team decisions forming.
   - **Days 61-90:** strategy and plan agreed, team changes, operating cadence.
9. **Operating cadence:** team meetings, reviews, communication rhythm.
10. **Personal management:** energy, advice network, reflection; how to get feedback.
11. **Check-ins:** with the boss at 30, 60 and 90 days; adjust the plan.

## Judgement calls
- **Turnaround:** move faster: stop the bleeding (cash, key customers, safety) in weeks, with a smaller listening tour.
- **Sustaining success:** go slower; learn why it works before changing anything; protect what's working.
- **Internal promotion:** reset relationships with former peers; signal the new role clearly.
- **Outsider:** culture learning is critical; find "cultural interpreters"; respect history.
- **Inherited commitments** (budgets, promises): understand them before reversing; be explicit about what changes.

## Red flags
- Prescribing before diagnosing.
- No explicit expectations agreement with the boss.
- Early wins that are cosmetic or contradict the desired culture.
- Avoiding team decisions past 90-120 days.
- Ignoring peers and informal influencers.

## Variants by context
- **CEO:** the board relationship, investor messaging, top-team composition, strategy refresh timing.
- **CFO / CRO / CHRO:** controls and compliance health check early; key-risk review.
- **Business-unit head:** P&L drivers, customer visits, frontline time.
- **Post-acquisition leader:** integration plan, retention of key talent, culture bridging.
- **Public-sector or regulated role:** statutory duties, stakeholder protocols, political environment.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.
- If key inputs are missing, ask up to 3 short questions, or proceed with clearly stated assumptions if the user wants speed.
- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers.
- Remind users not to paste confidential or personal data they are not permitted to share; suggest anonymising.
