# Worked example (illustrative; fictional multi-specialty day-care clinic, Tier-2 city)

**Inputs:**
- Fit-out and equipment: ₹1.8 Cr.
- Monthly fixed costs: ₹22 L (rent ₹4 L, salaried staff ₹13 L, utilities and maintenance ₹2.5 L, IT and admin ₹2.5 L).
- A second-shift step at >110 patients/day adds ₹6 L/month.
- Average net bill ₹1,400 (after the payer mix and insurer discounts).
- Variable cost 35% of the bill (consumables, doctor fee-share, payment and TPA fees) → contribution ₹910 per visit (65%).
- Working capital: insurer receivables of 45 days on 40% of revenue.
- Operating 26 days a month.

**Break-even:**
- ₹22 L ÷ ₹910 = **2,418 visits/month ≈ 93 per day**.

**Plausibility:** the existing clinic in a similar city averages 88 a day after 18 months, and capacity on one shift is 110 a day, so break-even sits at ~85% utilisation. **Too close.**

**Redesign test:** cut fixed costs to ₹18.5 L (a smaller footprint, rent ₹2.8 L; nurse rostering to demand) and raise the specialty mix (net bill ₹1,520, contribution ₹988).
- Break-even = **1,872 visits/month ≈ 72 per day** (65% of capacity).
- Expected at month 12: 90/day → **margin of safety 20%**.
- Operating leverage at 90/day ≈ 5x (profit is very volume-sensitive).

**Payback (redesigned; ramp 30 → 90/day over 12 months; computed with code; illustrative):**
- Cash trough ≈ ₹2.6 Cr in month 9 (fit-out + operating losses + receivables build).
- Simple payback ≈ month 34.
- Discounted payback (12%) ≈ month 40.

**Sensitivity:**
- Ramp 3 months slower → payback +5 months; trough +₹0.4 Cr.
- Net bill −5% → break-even 77/day.

**In plain words:** the clinic needs about 72 patients a day to cover its costs. We expect 90 by the end of year 1. That's a reasonable buffer only with the smaller footprint and the higher specialty mix. Plan funding for a cash dip of about ₹2.6 Cr.
