# Worked example (illustrative; fictional health insurer, South India focus)

**Question:** where is our right to win in retail health insurance in South India over the next 5 years?

| Resource / capability | Components | V | R | I | O | Result | Durability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 years of claims data (2.1M claims) | Data + actuarial team + coding standards | Yes: loss ratio 6 pts better than market in 3 states | Yes: new entrants lack history | Yes: history takes years to build | **No:** siloed; pricing and underwriting don't use it systematically | **Unused advantage** | 5+ yrs if used |
| Hospital network (1,200, with negotiated package rates) | Contracting team, rate cards, relationships | Yes: lower claim cost | Partly: 2 rivals have similar networks | Medium: 3-4 years to replicate | Yes | Temporary advantage | 3-4 yrs |
| Claims settlement speed (cashless approval in 45 min avg) | Process, rules engine, TPA management | Yes: a top choice driver in customer research | No: leaders now match it | Low | Yes | **Parity** | — |
| Agency distribution in Tier-2/3 towns (14,000 agents) | Recruitment, training, local trust | Yes: 70% of new business | Yes: few rivals have rural depth | High: social complexity, local trust | Partly: agent tech and incentives outdated | Sustained if organised | 5+ yrs (threat: digital aggregators) |
| Brand awareness | Advertising | Moderate | No | Low (money can buy it) | Yes | Parity | — |

**Implications:**
- **Fix "Organised" for the claims data:** a pricing analytics team embedded in underwriting; a data platform; incentives tied to loss-ratio outcomes. This is the highest-value move.
- **Protect and modernise agency distribution:** an agent app, faster commissions, AI-assisted needs analysis. Defend against aggregators by serving segments they reach poorly.
- **Network:** exploit while it lasts; deepen exclusive arrangements in key cities.
- **Claims speed and brand:** run efficiently; don't over-invest beyond parity.
