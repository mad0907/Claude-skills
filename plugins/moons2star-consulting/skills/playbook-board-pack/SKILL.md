# Playbook: Board Pack

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** a board pack directors can read in 20 minutes and decide on confidently, with the hard questions already answered, the governance boxes ticked, and a resolution ready for the minutes.

## The senior standard
1. **The decision is explicit:** approve, note, or guide, with the exact resolution wording.
2. **Directors' duties shape the paper.** Give them what they need to exercise independent judgement and care: options, risks, assumptions, dissent, conflicts.
3. **Short main paper, rich annexes.** About 4-8 pages plus annexes; an executive summary that stands alone.
4. **Governance compliance is checked,** not assumed: approval thresholds, committee routing, related-party rules, disclosure obligations, conflicts.
5. **Anticipate challenge.** Rehearse the 10-15 most likely director questions; answer them in the paper or the Q&A annex.
6. **Honest risk and downside.** Boards punish surprises, not candour.
7. **Pre-brief key directors** (committee chairs, the lead independent director) before the meeting.

## Before you start: diagnose
- The item: decision / approval / noting / discussion; the proposal and why now.
- The board or committee, meeting date and paper deadline (often 7 days before).
- Approval requirements: the delegation-of-authority matrix, statutory requirements, shareholder approval if needed.
- Directors: backgrounds, known concerns, past positions; conflicts.
- Material available: business case, analyses, legal opinions, committee recommendations.

**Governance checklist (adapt to jurisdiction and constitution):**
- Is the board or committee the right authority? Is committee review needed first (audit, risk, nomination and remuneration, CSR)?
- Statutory or shareholder approval required?
- Related-party transaction: audit committee approval; the interested directors recuse themselves.
- Conflicts of interest declared.
- Disclosure obligations (stock exchange, regulator) and timing.
- Quorum and notice requirements.
- Legal opinion obtained where needed.
- Minutes: the draft resolution and the key discussion points to record.

## Judgement calls
- **Noting vs decision:** don't hide decisions in noting items. Boards should knowingly approve.
- **Dissent:** if management or a committee is divided, say so and give both views.
- **Price-sensitive matters:** confidentiality, insider lists, disclosure timing.
- **Late changes:** a supplementary paper with the changes highlighted; avoid tabling papers at the meeting.
- **Repeat items:** reference prior decisions; show what's changed.

## Red flags
- A resolution missing or vague.
- 40-page papers with no summary.
- No options or downside case.
- Governance routing not checked; related-party issues missed.
- Directors surprised by a known issue.
- Slides that repeat the paper.

## Variants by context
- **Audit or risk committee:** control findings, risk metrics, remediation; independence.
- **M&A or major capex:** valuation, financing, integration, downside protection; fairness or valuation opinions.
- **Policy approvals (risk appetite, AI policy, ESG):** policy text, changes from the last version, impact.
- **Regulated entities:** regulator-mandated approvals and reporting (e.g. RBI or IRDAI requirements).
- **Unlisted or family companies:** shareholder agreements and reserved matters.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.
- If key inputs are missing, ask up to 3 short questions, or proceed with clearly stated assumptions if the user wants speed.
- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers.
- Remind users not to paste confidential or personal data they are not permitted to share; suggest anonymising.
