# Scenario Planning

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** a disciplined way to make decisions when the future cannot be forecast. You get several plausible futures, a test of today's strategy against each, a set of moves that work regardless, and early-warning signposts that tell you which future is arriving.

## The senior standard
1. **Scenarios serve a decision.** Start with a focal question ("Should we commit ₹2,000 Cr to a new plant by 2030?"), not "the future of the industry".
2. **Uncertainties, not trends.** Demographics or committed regulation are predetermined: include them in every scenario. Axes must be genuinely uncertain *and* high-impact.
3. **Plausible, not probable.** Scenarios are not best/base/worst. Each must be internally consistent and challenging, and none should be the "official future".
4. **Quantify the implications:** demand, prices, margins, capital needs in each world, so strategy can be tested.
5. **Robustness over prediction.** Look for no-regret moves, options (small bets that can be scaled), and hedges.
6. **Signposts make it live.** Define observable indicators and thresholds that signal which scenario is unfolding, and review them on a fixed rhythm.

## Before you start: diagnose
- The focal decision, horizon (5-15 years typical) and decision-makers.
- Current strategy and its key assumptions.
- Available inputs: PESTEL scan, industry analysis, expert views, data.
- Who should take part. Scenario work benefits from diverse perspectives, including sceptics and outsiders.

## Method
1. **Focal question and horizon.**
2. **Driving forces:** brainstorm 20-40 across PESTEL, industry, technology and customer behaviour.
3. **Classify each:** predetermined (high certainty) vs uncertain; rate impact on the focal question.
4. **Select two critical uncertainties:** high impact, high uncertainty and **independent of each other** (test: can both extremes combine plausibly?). Define clear polar outcomes for each.
5. **Build the 2×2.** Give each scenario a memorable name and write a short narrative of how the world got there. Include the predetermined trends in every scenario.
6. **Quantify:** key variables per scenario (market size, price, cost, regulation, technology adoption), plus implications for the business: revenue, margin and capital.
7. **Stress-test the current strategy:** performance in each scenario (works / struggles / fails), and why.
8. **Identify moves:**
   - **No-regret:** good in all scenarios.
   - **Options:** low-cost moves that create the right to scale if a scenario emerges (pilots, partnerships, land, licences).
   - **Hedges:** limit damage in adverse scenarios (contract terms, flexible capacity, diversification).
   - **Big bets:** only if you choose to back one scenario. Make the bet explicit.
9. **Signposts and triggers:** 2-4 indicators per scenario, with thresholds and the pre-agreed action when crossed.
10. **Governance:** review signposts quarterly or semi-annually; refresh the scenarios every 1-2 years or on major shocks.

## Judgement calls
- **Avoid a "middle" scenario.** Teams gravitate to it and ignore the others. If four are too many, use three plus a "wildcard".
- **Correlated axes produce two meaningful scenarios and two impossible ones.** Pick different axes.
- **Wildcards** (low probability, high impact: pandemic, war, a technology leap) are better treated as stress tests than as axes.
- **Narratives persuade; numbers decide.** You need both.
- **Use scenarios to prompt decisions now:** which options to buy today, and which commitments to defer.

## Red flags
- Best / base / worst cases labelled as scenarios.
- Axes that are trends, or correlated.
- Stories with no numbers and no strategic implications.
- No signposts; scenarios filed after the workshop.
- Strategy unchanged after the exercise.

## Variants by context
- **Energy transition / climate:** carbon price path × technology cost curves; policy stringency; physical-risk scenarios. Align with recognised climate scenario sets for disclosure (e.g. under IFRS S2).
- **Technology and AI disruption:** the speed of capability gains × the regulatory or adoption response.
- **Geopolitics and trade:** openness of trade × supply-chain regionalisation.
- **Financial services:** rate path × credit cycle; digital competition × regulation.
- **Healthcare:** payer models × technology adoption; public-funding levels.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.
- If key inputs are missing, ask up to 3 short questions, or proceed with clearly stated assumptions if the user wants speed.
- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers.
- Remind users not to paste confidential or personal data they are not permitted to share; suggest anonymising.
