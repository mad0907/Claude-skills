# Worked example (illustrative; quick-commerce grocery in India's top 8 metros, perspective of an investor considering a growth-stage player, horizon 2026-2030)

| Force | Rating | Drivers and evidence | Trend | Signposts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rivalry | **High** | 4-5 well-funded players; heavy discounting; high fixed costs of dark-store networks; low differentiation on core grocery | Easing slightly as funding tightens and players focus on contribution margin; consolidation possible | Discount depth; store-opening pace; funding rounds |
| New entrants | **Medium** | High capital needs and density economics deter small entrants; but large e-commerce and retail groups can enter with existing supply chains | Stable; large incumbents' entry is the main risk | Launch announcements by large retailers |
| Buyer power (consumers) | **Medium-High** | Zero switching costs; multi-app usage; price comparison is easy | Stable; loyalty programmes may raise switching costs slightly | Membership uptake; multi-homing rates |
| Supplier power | **Low-Medium** | FMCG brands need the channel for impulse and urban reach; some large brands negotiate hard; private labels growing | Supplier power falls as quick commerce becomes a key channel | Ad-revenue share; private-label share |
| Substitutes | **Medium** | Neighbourhood stores (fast, credit, relationships); scheduled e-grocery; modern trade | Neighbourhood stores digitising, so substitute strength rises | Neighbourhood-store app adoption |

**Shaping factors:**
- Regulation on dark stores (local zoning) and foreign-investment rules for inventory models.
- Retail-media advertising as a complement and profit source.
- AI-driven demand forecasting lowering waste.

**Profitability cross-check:** most players report negative EBITDA, though improving contribution margins in mature cities. This is consistent with high rivalry and buyer power. Mature dark stores reportedly reach positive contribution (company disclosures; verify).

**Profit pools:** advertising and private labels are more profitable than delivery fees; dense metro catchments beat expansion cities.

**Implications for the investor:**
- Back players with leading density in the top 3-4 metros, rising advertising and private-label share, and discipline on new-city expansion.
- Watch for the entry of large retail groups.
- **Go**, with conditions on unit-economics milestones (contribution per order positive in ≥70% of stores).
