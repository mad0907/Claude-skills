# Five Forces

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** a clear explanation of why an industry is as profitable as it is, how that will change, and what a specific player should do about it: where to compete, how to position, and which forces to try to influence.

## The senior standard
1. **Define the industry precisely:** product scope and geography. "Indian cement" behaves very differently from "cement in South India"; regional markets have different rivalry.
2. **Drivers, not adjectives.** Each force is rated from structural evidence (concentration, switching costs, scale economies, capital requirements, differentiation, buyer concentration, input substitutability).
3. **Cross-check with profits.** Compare the conclusion with actual industry margins and returns on capital over a cycle. If the forces say "unattractive" but returns are high, re-examine.
4. **Regulation, complements and technology** are not forces, but they shape forces. Include them explicitly.
5. **Dynamics matter most.** Strategy depends on how forces will shift (e.g. AI lowering entry barriers, consolidation raising buyer power).
6. **Implications for a specific player.** The analysis should end in positioning and actions, not a generic description.

## Before you start: diagnose
- The industry definition and the player whose perspective matters (incumbent, entrant, investor, supplier).
- The horizon (3-5 years typical).
- Available data: market shares, margins, capacity, cost structures, switching costs, regulatory changes.

## Method
1. **Define the industry** (product and geography) and its value chain position.
2. **For each force, list drivers and evidence:**
   - **Rivalry:** number and balance of competitors, industry growth, fixed costs and capacity utilisation, product differentiation, exit barriers, price competition patterns.
   - **Threat of new entrants:** scale economies, capital requirements, access to distribution, regulatory barriers and licences, brand and switching costs, incumbent retaliation, technology shifts lowering barriers.
   - **Buyer power:** buyer concentration, volume share, switching costs, price sensitivity, backward-integration threat, information availability.
   - **Supplier power:** supplier concentration, uniqueness of inputs, switching costs, forward-integration threat, importance of the industry to suppliers.
   - **Substitutes:** price-performance of alternatives, switching costs, buyer propensity to switch.
3. **Rate each force** Low / Medium / High, with a one-line justification.
4. **Profitability cross-check:** industry margins and ROCE over a cycle; reconcile with the ratings.
5. **Shaping factors:** regulation, complements, technology (including AI and digital platforms), ESG requirements.
6. **Trends:** how each force will change over the horizon, and why; signposts.
7. **Implications:** where profit pools are and will be; positioning (cost leadership, differentiation, focus); actions to shape forces (consolidation, switching costs, partnerships, vertical moves); go or no-go for entrants and investors.

## Judgement calls
- **Segment-level analysis** often reveals attractive niches in unattractive industries.
- **Platforms and ecosystems** change the forces (network effects raise entry barriers; aggregators raise buyer power). Analyse the platform's role.
- **Government as a buyer or price-setter** (e.g. healthcare tariffs, public procurement) is a powerful buyer force.
- **Don't confuse growth with attractiveness.** Fast-growing markets can have intense rivalry and low returns.

## Red flags
- An industry defined too broadly.
- Ratings without evidence; no profitability cross-check.
- No trends; a static snapshot.
- Generic implications ("compete on quality").

## Variants by context
- **Investor screening:** focus on durability of returns and on threats to margins.
- **Entrant:** focus on entry barriers, incumbent retaliation and niche entry points.
- **Incumbent:** focus on shaping forces and defending the profit pool.
- **Regulated sectors:** regulator as a quasi-force (tariffs, licences, mandates).
- **Digital markets:** network effects, data advantages, multi-homing costs.
