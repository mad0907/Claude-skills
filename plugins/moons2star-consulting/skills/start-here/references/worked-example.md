# Worked examples (illustrative)

## 1. "Our CEO wants to know if we should launch a subscription service for our appliance customers. Board meeting in 3 weeks."

**Your route:** a go or no-go recommendation on an appliance-care subscription, for the CEO and then the board, in 3 weeks.

| Step | Skill | What it produces | Why now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `problem-statement` | A sharp question, success criteria, scope | Avoids solving the wrong problem |
| 2 | `jobs-to-be-done` + `interview-guide` | Customer needs and an interview plan (10-12 customers) | Demand evidence is the biggest unknown |
| 3 | `market-sizing` | Serviceable market and an adoption range | Sizes the prize |
| 4 | `pricing-strategy` + `unit-economics` | Price tiers; contribution per subscriber; payback | Tests whether the economics work |
| 5 | `business-case` + `sensitivity-analysis` | NPV, IRR and the drivers that break it | The investment decision |
| 6 | `red-team-review` | Severity-ranked challenges | Before the CEO sees it |
| 7 | `playbook-board-pack` | Board paper, resolution, director Q&A | The board submission |

**Checks:** verify market figures with `source-validation`; Finance to review the model.

**Expert review:** consumer-protection and auto-renewal rules need legal review.

**Shall I start step 1?** Share what you know about the idea, your customer base size and any past attempts.

## 2. "I need to tell my boss the project is delayed."

**Your route:** one skill, `executive-email`, using the bad-news variant: the delay and impact in the first line, the cause, the recovery plan, and any decision needed.

**Shall I draft it?** Tell me the project, the new date, the reason, and what you're doing about it.
