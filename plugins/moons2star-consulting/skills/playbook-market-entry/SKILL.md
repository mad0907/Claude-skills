# Playbook: Market Entry

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** a disciplined go or no-go, and the right where and how: markets ranked on facts, a proposition customers want, an entry mode matched to risk and capability, and a staged plan that limits the downside.

## The senior standard
1. **Attractiveness and ability to win, both.** A big market where you have no edge is a trap; a small one where you win can be a beachhead.
2. **Screen broadly, then go deep.** Rank many candidates on a few criteria; deep-dive on 2-3.
3. **Regulation and the local environment** often decide feasibility: licensing, foreign-ownership limits, tax, data localisation, labour rules.
4. **Customers and competitors on the ground.** Interviews, field visits and local partners beat desk research alone.
5. **The entry mode fits the risk, capability and control needed:** organic, partnership, licence or franchise, JV, acquisition.
6. **Stage the commitment.** A test market or pilot city, with clear gates before scaling.
7. **Exit criteria defined upfront:** what would make us stop.

## Before you start: diagnose
- The entry objective (growth, diversification, following customers, defending against competitors); the horizon; ambition.
- Candidate markets or segments; existing presence; capabilities and assets that travel.
- Constraints: capital, management bandwidth, risk appetite, regulatory limits.
- Decision-makers and timeline.

## Judgement calls
- **Psychic distance** (language, culture, business practice) raises cost and risk. Partners can bridge it.
- **Follow-the-customer entries** (serving existing clients abroad) are lower risk, but may limit local growth.
- **Regulated sectors:** the licence timeline is often the critical path; build it into the plan.
- **Acquisition vs organic:** speed and local capability vs integration risk and price.
- **Pilot design:** choose a test market representative of the larger opportunity, with measurable gates.

## Red flags
- Choosing a market for its size alone.
- Desk research only; no customer contact.
- Regulatory feasibility checked late.
- One entry mode considered.
- A full-scale launch with no gates.
- No exit criteria; sunk-cost escalation.

## Variants by context
- **International (cross-border):** foreign-investment rules, tax treaties, repatriation, currency, transfer pricing, data rules.
- **New Indian state or city:** state-level licences, local language, distribution partners, state incentives.
- **New segment (e.g. SME after enterprise):** product simplification, channel change, cost-to-serve.
- **New category:** capability gaps, brand stretch, cannibalisation.
- **Digital or platform entry:** network effects, customer-acquisition costs, local competitors.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.
- If key inputs are missing, ask up to 3 short questions, or proceed with clearly stated assumptions if the user wants speed.
- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers.
- Remind users not to paste confidential or personal data they are not permitted to share; suggest anonymising.
