# Worked example (illustrative; payroll software churn study, N = 16: 6 churned, 4 at-risk, 4 healthy, 2 lost deals)

**Bottom line:**
- Churn is driven by a bad first 60 days, specifically the first payroll error that reaches employees, more than by price or features.
- The evidence favours investing in onboarding, plus 2 product fixes, over feature development.
- **Confidence:** moderate (small sample; validate with ticket data).

| Theme | Overall | Churned (6) | At-risk (4) | Healthy (4) | Lost (2) | Intensity | Typical quote |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The first employee-visible payroll error triggered the decision to leave or doubt us | 9/14* | 5/6 | 3/4 | 1/4 | n/a | Very high | "Once salaries went wrong, the CFO stopped trusting the system." (churned, finance head) |
| Onboarding took longer than promised; no single point of contact | 10/14* | 6/6 | 3/4 | 1/4 | n/a | High | "We were passed between four people." (at-risk, HR head) |
| Multi-state statutory set-up was confusing | 8/16 | 4/6 | 2/4 | 1/4 | 1/2 | High | "Each state's deductions took us weeks to get right." (churned) |
| Price was a reason to leave | 3/16 | 2/6 | 0/4 | 0/4 | 1/2 | Medium | — |
| Healthy customers had an internal payroll expert who learned the product deeply | 4/4 healthy vs 1/6 churned | 1/6 | 1/4 | 4/4 | n/a | — | "Our payroll lead became the in-house champion." (healthy) |

*Excludes the 2 lost deals (never onboarded).

**Numbers from interviews (indicative):**
- Days to first correct payroll: churned median 52 (range 35-80, n=6); healthy median 24 (range 18-30, n=4).
- Hours per month on fixes: churned median 18 (n=5).

**Contradictions and outliers:**
- One healthy customer had a poor onboarding but stayed. Their payroll head had used us at a previous employer, a sign that an expert champion can compensate.
- One churned customer left after an acquisition (a parent-company mandate). This is a non-addressable churn reason.

**Hypotheses:**
- Onboarding predicts churn: **supported** (strong pattern across segments).
- Price drives churn: **refuted** for most respondents.
- Multi-state complexity: **nuanced** (matters most for companies in 3+ states).

**Implications:**
- Dedicated onboarding specialist for the first 60 days, with a target of the first correct payroll in ≤30 days.
- Product fixes: a guided multi-state statutory set-up; a payroll pre-check that catches errors before payslips are issued.
- Champion programme: train one customer-side payroll expert.

**Confidence and gaps:**
- Small sample; no respondents under 100 employees.
- **Validate:** correlate days-to-first-correct-payroll and first-60-day tickets with churn across all customers (CRM and support data).
