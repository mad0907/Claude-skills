# Operating Model Design

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** a coherent target model linked to strategy, showing how work flows, who decides, where it's done, what it costs and how to get there, with the structural options and trade-offs made explicit.

## The senior standard
1. **Strategy drives design.** Translate strategy into 5-8 design principles (e.g. "customer decisions close to the market", "one global data platform", "scale functions in GCCs") and use them to judge options.
2. **More than an org chart.** Structure is one of eight elements; most failures come from unclear decision rights, misaligned incentives or broken processes.
3. **Decision rights are the core.** For the 10-20 decisions that matter, state who decides, who recommends, who must agree, who gives input and who executes.
4. **Options, not a single answer.** Compare 2-3 structures (functional, business-unit or product, matrix, hub-and-spoke, platform) against the principles.
5. **Spans and layers matter.** Too many layers slow decisions; very narrow spans waste cost. Use benchmarks as indicators, not rules.
6. **Location and sourcing are strategic:** capability depth, talent markets, risk, regulatory constraints, and cost.
7. **Transition is part of design:** sequencing, people impact, controls during change.

## Before you start: diagnose
- Strategy and the reason for change (growth, cost, control, capability, an integration).
- Current model across the eight elements; pain points (slow decisions, duplication, gaps).
- Constraints: regulation, labour law, works councils, contracts, systems, budget.
- Stakeholders and their concerns.

## Method
1. **Design principles (5-8)** from strategy and pain points; agree them with the sponsor.
2. **Current-state assessment** across eight elements:
   - value and services;
   - processes and capabilities;
   - structure and roles;
   - decision rights and governance;
   - locations and sourcing;
   - technology and data;
   - performance and incentives;
   - culture and ways of working.
   Include evidence (spans, layers, cost, cycle times, surveys).
3. **Capability map:** which capabilities are differentiating (keep close, invest), which are scale or commodity (consolidate, shared service, GCC, outsource), and which are missing.
4. **Structural options (2-3):** describe each; evaluate them against the principles, cost, risk and transition complexity; pick one.
5. **Target design:** high-level structure; key roles and accountabilities; spans and layers; interfaces (service catalogues, SLAs); governance forums.
6. **Decision-rights matrix** for the key decisions (see `raci-builder`).
7. **Locations and sourcing:** what is done where (onshore, nearshore, GCC, partner), with criteria and risk.
8. **Technology and data enablers:** platforms, data ownership, automation and AI roles.
9. **Performance and incentives:** KPIs per unit; how targets cascade; incentive alignment.
10. **Sizing:** headcount and cost, current vs target; one-off transition costs; benefits.
11. **Transition plan:** waves, people process (selection, redeployment, consultation), control continuity, communication; risks.

## Judgement calls
- **Matrix structures** need very clear decision rights and conflict-resolution mechanisms, or they create paralysis.
- **Centralise what benefits from scale and standardisation;** keep close to the market what needs speed and customer intimacy.
- **GCCs succeed** when they own outcomes, products and capabilities, not just tasks. Design for an evolution from transactions to expertise.
- **Culture eats structure.** Plan behaviours, rituals and leadership role-modelling, not just boxes.
- **People-impact decisions** must follow legal and ethical processes; communicate early and with respect.

## Red flags
- An org chart with no process, decision-rights or incentive design.
- Design principles written after the design.
- A single option presented.
- Spans of 2-3 across management, or 8+ layers.
- No transition plan or people process.
- Location chosen on cost alone.

## Variants by context
- **GCC / shared services:** service catalogue, SLAs, chargeback model, governance with business units, career paths, a maturity roadmap (transactional → expertise → innovation).
- **AI and data function:** hub-and-spoke (central platform, standards and governance; embedded product teams), product ownership, a model-risk function.
- **Post-merger:** day-1 model vs target model; leadership selection; integration of cultures and systems.
- **Function redesign (finance, HR, IT):** business partnering, centres of excellence, shared services, and digital and AI enablement.
- **Public sector:** statutory roles; accountability to ministers or boards; procurement constraints.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.

- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers.
