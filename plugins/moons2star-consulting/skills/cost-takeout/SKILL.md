# Cost Take-out

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** a cost programme that delivers real, lasting savings without damaging what drives revenue, quality or safety. Every rupee is traceable from idea to the budget line where it is banked.

## The senior standard
1. **Baseline before ideas.** A reconciled, agreed cost baseline (ties to the P&L) by category, function, site and driver. Arguments about savings are usually arguments about the baseline.
2. **Cut costs by managing the drivers, not by percentage.** Across-the-board cuts punish the efficient and return within 18 months. Address demand (what we do), price (what we pay), process (how we do it), structure (who and where) and technology (automation and AI).
3. **Protect what grows the business.** Explicitly ring-fence spend that drives revenue, quality, safety, compliance and critical capability.
4. **Run-rate vs in-year vs cost-to-achieve.** Distinguish annualised run-rate savings, savings realised this year, and one-off costs (severance, systems, consultants).
5. **Pipeline above target.** Aim for 120-130% of target identified, because slippage is normal.
6. **Bank it.** Savings count only when removed from budgets or forecasts, with Finance validating them. Otherwise costs creep back.
7. **People with dignity.** Where roles are affected, follow legal process, consult early, prefer redeployment and natural attrition, and communicate honestly.

## Before you start: diagnose
- Target (₹ and %), timeframe, and why (margin, competitiveness, funding growth, a crisis).
- Cost base: P&L by nature and function; headcount by function and location; third-party spend by category and supplier; capex.
- Benchmarks available (internal across units, external).
- Constraints and protected areas; labour law and union context; contracts with lock-ins.
- Governance: sponsor, PMO, Finance validation.

## Method
1. **Baseline:** reconcile to the P&L; segment by function, category, site and controllability; identify cost drivers (volume, headcount, complexity, policy).
2. **Diagnose:** benchmarks (cost as % revenue, per unit, per FTE, spans and layers); internal variation between units; spend cube (category × supplier × business unit); activity analysis for support functions.
3. **Generate levers** across five types:
   - **Demand:** stop or reduce activities, reports, SKUs, service levels, policies (travel, fleet), and complexity.
   - **Price:** renegotiate, consolidate suppliers, specification changes, should-cost models, payment terms.
   - **Process:** lean redesign, standardisation, first-time-right, self-service.
   - **Structure:** spans and layers, location (shared services or GCC), outsourcing or insourcing, footprint consolidation.
   - **Technology and AI:** automation, AI-assisted work, decommissioning legacy systems, cloud cost optimisation.
   - **Zero-based challenge** for discretionary categories: rebuild from need, not last year.
4. **Size each initiative:** formula and assumptions; run-rate saving; in-year saving by month; cost-to-achieve; owner; dependencies; risk to revenue, quality, safety and people.
5. **Prioritise:** value, ease, time and risk; quick wins (0-3 months), medium (3-12), structural (12+).
6. **Pipeline management:** stage gates (L0 idea → L1 validated → L2 approved plan → L3 implemented → L4 banked in budget), each with defined evidence; weekly tracking.
7. **People and change:** impact assessment; legal and HR process; consultation; redeployment; communications; retention of critical talent.
8. **Lock in:** budget reductions; policy changes; headcount controls; supplier-contract changes; KPIs per initiative; post-programme review at 12 months to check for cost creep.

## Judgement calls
- **Cost-to-serve thinking.** Sometimes the biggest saving is in complexity (SKUs, customer customisations, report requests), which needs commercial decisions, not just operations.
- **Price levers are fast but finite;** structural and process levers are slower but durable. Balance the portfolio.
- **Avoid cutting maintenance, safety, cyber or compliance** to hit a number. These risks surface later at a higher cost.
- **Use savings to fund growth** where possible ("fuel for growth"). It helps morale and strategy.
- **Measure the unintended effects:** service levels, employee engagement, attrition of key people, customer complaints.

## Red flags
- No reconciled baseline; savings measured against the budget rather than actuals.
- Across-the-board % cuts.
- No cost-to-achieve, or severance excluded.
- Savings "identified" but never removed from budgets.
- Initiatives with no owners; pipeline at 100% of target or less.
- Revenue-generating or safety-critical spend cut without assessment.

## Variants by context
- **Manufacturing:** conversion cost, yield, energy, maintenance strategy, procurement of raw materials, logistics.
- **Services / IT / GCC:** pyramid and span optimisation, location strategy, automation and AI, vendor rationalisation, cloud FinOps.
- **Retail:** store labour scheduling, shrinkage, rent renegotiation, supply-chain network, assortment.
- **Banks / insurers:** branch network, digital migration, operations centres, technology run-cost, procurement.
- **Post-merger:** overlap elimination, systems consolidation, footprint, procurement scale. Track synergies separately from base-business savings.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.
- If key inputs are missing, ask up to 3 short questions, or proceed with clearly stated assumptions if the user wants speed.
- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers.
- Remind users not to paste confidential or personal data they are not permitted to share; suggest anonymising.
