# Interview Guide

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** interviews that produce evidence you can use: real behaviour, real numbers and honest views, in a consistent format you can synthesise, instead of polite chats that confirm what you already believed.

## The senior standard
1. **Objectives first.** 3-5 learning objectives tied to the decision. Every question serves one of them; anything else is cut.
2. **The right people, enough of them.** Cover the variety that matters (segments, roles, regions, users vs buyers, fans vs churned). Typically 5-8 per segment for patterns; 12-20 overall for a study.
3. **Past behaviour beats opinion.** "Tell me about the last time you…" is evidence; "Would you use…?" is speculation. People are poor predictors of their own future behaviour and politely over-positive.
4. **Funnel structure.** Start broad and open, then narrow to specifics and quantification; test your hypotheses last, so you don't contaminate earlier answers.
5. **Neutral wording.** No leading, double-barrelled or loaded questions. Don't pitch the idea.
6. **Quantify where possible:** frequency, volumes, spend, time, switching costs, ranges.
7. **Ethics and compliance:** informed consent, confidentiality, data protection, no material non-public or confidential information from experts.

## Before you start: diagnose
- The decision and hypotheses; what you already know.
- Interview type: expert, customer, employee, executive, partner, reference.
- Who: segments, roles, how many; recruitment route (own customers, panel, expert network, referrals).
- Constraints: time per call (30-60 min), language, recording permission, incentives.

## Method
1. **Learning objectives** (3-5), each linked to the decision.
2. **Sample design:** segments × number; include critics, lost customers and non-users, not only fans.
3. **Screener:** 4-6 qualifying questions (role, responsibility, recency of experience, company size, conflicts of interest).
4. **Opening (3-5 min):** purpose (without revealing confidential intent), consent and recording, confidentiality, "no right answers", time check.
5. **Context (5-10 min):** role, responsibilities, the environment.
6. **Core sections (one per objective):**
   - Open question → probes (what, how, why, example, numbers) → the specific detail.
   - Behaviour-based: "Walk me through the last time…"
   - Quantification: "Roughly how often / how much / how long?"
   - Pain and workaround: "What's hardest? What have you tried? What did it cost?"
7. **Hypothesis testing (late):** concept or claim reactions, with neutral framing; ask what would make them pay or switch, and what would stop them.
8. **Close (3-5 min):** "What should I have asked?"; referrals; thanks; next steps.
9. **Time plan:** minutes per section; mark which questions to drop if short of time.
10. **Note template:** respondent ID | segment | key quotes (verbatim) | facts and numbers | behaviours | pains | surprises | hypothesis evidence (for / against).
11. **Compliance (expert calls):** a pre-call disclaimer; no current-employer confidential information; no price-sensitive information; stop if a respondent strays.

## Judgement calls
- **Expert vs customer:** experts give an industry view and estimates (verify them); customers give needs and behaviour. Use both.
- **The interviewer's opinion is invisible.** Don't react to answers; probe neutrally ("Say more about that").
- **Silence works.** Pause after answers; respondents often add the most useful detail.
- **Pivot when the pattern is clear.** After 5-6 interviews in a segment, adjust the guide to probe new themes.
- **Senior executives:** fewer questions, more strategic; prepare tightly; respect time.

## Red flags
- "Would you buy…?" as the main evidence.
- Leading or double-barrelled questions.
- Only friendly customers or internal champions interviewed.
- No quantification; no note template.
- Expert calls without compliance guardrails.

## Variants by context
- **Customer discovery / jobs-to-be-done:** last-purchase or switch timeline (first thought → search → choice → use).
- **Win-loss:** decision process, alternatives considered, decisive factors, price perception.
- **Employee listening:** psychological safety; confidentiality and anonymised reporting; avoid manager presence.
- **Diligence reference calls:** relationship, satisfaction, risks, switching intent, share of wallet.
- **Stakeholder interviews (change or strategy):** priorities, concerns, success measures, influence map.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.
- If key inputs are missing, ask up to 3 short questions, or proceed with clearly stated assumptions if the user wants speed.
- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers.
- Remind users not to paste confidential or personal data they are not permitted to share; suggest anonymising.
