# Worked example (illustrative; EV charging in India for an investment committee: 6 reports, 4 filings, 5 expert calls)

**Bottom line:**
- Public EV charging returns are driven by utilisation, not network size.
- Only highway, fleet-depot and dense urban-hub sites reach the ~15% utilisation needed for attractive returns today.
- A focused fleet-and-highway operator is investable; a broad urban-network play is not, yet.
- **Confidence:** moderate.

### Finding 1: Site utilisation above ~15% is the threshold for mid-teens project IRR; most urban public chargers run at 5-8%
- **Evidence:**
  - Two listed operators' disclosures (FY26): network-average utilisation 6-9%.
  - Operator investor presentation: highway sites 18-24%.
  - 3 expert calls: a break-even of ~11-13% on fast chargers (est.).
- **Strength:** moderate (disclosures + experts; definitions of utilisation vary).
- **Contradictions:** one industry report claims 20% average utilisation. It defines utilisation as hours with any session, not energy delivered. That definitional difference explains the gap.
- **So what:** screen targets on site mix and utilisation by site type, not on charger count.

### Finding 2: Fleet electrification (ride-hailing, last-mile delivery) provides anchor demand that public consumer demand doesn't yet
- **Evidence:** fleet-operator EV adoption targets; e-3W and e-4W fleet registrations growing faster than private cars (vehicle-registration data); 2 depot-charging operators' contracts.
- **Strength:** strong.
- **So what:** prefer operators with long-term fleet contracts (take-or-pay).

### Finding 3: Grid connection costs and timelines are the main execution risk
- **Evidence:** 4 of 5 experts cited 6-14 month delays for high-capacity connections; state regulatory orders on connection charges vary.
- **Strength:** moderate.
- **So what:** diligence should test each target's pipeline for secured grid connections.

### Finding 4: Policy support is shifting from vehicle purchase subsidies to infrastructure incentives
- **Evidence:** central scheme notifications (verify the current scheme), and state EV policies.
- **Strength:** strong (official texts), but the incentive amounts change frequently.
- **So what:** model cases without incentives, and treat incentives as upside.

**Open questions:**
- Real utilisation by site type for the 3 targets → data request in diligence.
- Battery-swapping substitution risk for 2- and 3-wheelers → 2 more expert calls.

**Parked:** consumer-sentiment surveys on range anxiety (not decision-critical for a fleet-led thesis).
