# Worked example (illustrative; fictional Indian pharmaceutical company and fictional consulting team)

**Situation:** A consulting team has drafted a growth plan for a mid-sized Indian pharmaceutical company. The brief is to grow sales of its prescription pain-relief brand (a Schedule H1 product with dependence risk) by 25% in 18 months, and to win a hospital-supply tender in a neighbouring country. The engagement partner runs the integrity check before the steering committee.

**Plan as drafted (summarised):**
1. Rank doctors by current prescription volume; focus field force on the top 10% of prescribers.
2. Invite the top 200 prescribers to a "knowledge summit" in a resort abroad, all costs paid, with spouses.
3. Promote the brand for post-operative pain in children, "where competitors are absent". The approved label covers adults only.
4. Raise dose-strength mix: message that the higher strength "works faster with no added risk".
5. For the tender, appoint a "local facilitator" recommended by the hospital authority's procurement head, paid a 6% success fee, invoiced through a company in a third country.
6. Use competitor price lists provided by a newly hired sales manager from a rival.
7. Partner's note on the draft: "Keep items 2 and 5 out of the written deck; discuss verbally."

## Integrity Check: Brand growth and tender plan, for steering committee
**Overall verdict:** **Stop** in its current form. Five items need redesign; two can proceed with conditions.
**Countries and sector:** India and neighbouring country; pharmaceuticals; public-sector hospital buyer. Not a legal opinion.

| # | Item | Red-line area | Evidence that triggered the flag | Verdict | Lawful alternative or condition | Reviewer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Target top 10% of prescribers | Health products; vulnerable groups | Growth depends on highest prescribers of a dependence-risk product | **Escalate** | Segment by unmet clinical need and appropriate use, not volume; add safety and responsible-use messaging; monitor for unusual prescribing | Medical affairs; compliance |
| 2 | Resort "knowledge summit" with spouses | Bribery (doctors); UCPMP 2024 | All-expenses foreign travel and hospitality for top prescribers | **Stop** | Accredited continuing medical education in India, on clinical content, open selection, no leisure or spouse costs, within UCPMP limits | Compliance; legal |
| 3 | Paediatric post-operative promotion | Health products (unapproved use) | Label covers adults only; plan targets children | **Stop** | Do not promote. If there is clinical evidence, pursue a label extension through the regulator | Regulatory affairs; legal |
| 4 | "No added risk" claim for higher strength | Health products (misleading claim) | Claim not supported by approved product information | **Stop** | Use only claims in the approved prescribing information, balanced with safety data | Medical affairs |
| 5 | Facilitator with 6% success fee via third country | Bribery (public officials); third parties; money laundering | Recommended by the buyer's procurement head; success fee; payment through an unrelated jurisdiction | **Stop** | Bid directly or through a distributor selected by documented due diligence, fixed fee for defined services, payment to a local account in the provider's name, audit rights and anti-bribery clauses | Legal; compliance; board audit committee |
| 6 | Rival's price lists from new hire | Confidential information; competition | Data came from a former employer, not a public source | **Stop** | Quarantine and delete through legal; use public tender results and market research instead; brief the new hire on confidentiality | Legal |
| 7 | "Keep items 2 and 5 out of the deck" | Records and obstruction | Instruction to avoid written record of high-risk items | **Escalate** (immediately) | Record all items and this review in writing; preserve drafts; route through counsel | General counsel; engagement risk partner |
| 8 | Field-force training on the brand | — | Standard, approved-label content | **Clear** | Keep | — |
| 9 | Pricing-tier review for hospital channel | Competition (low) | Uses public tender data only | **Proceed with conditions** | No contact with competitors on pricing; document data sources | Legal (light review) |

**Conditions to proceed:** items 2-6 redesigned as above; item 1 reframed around appropriate use; written record of this review held by the engagement risk partner.

**Questions to answer before work continues:**
- Who introduced the facilitator, and has any payment or promise already been made?
- Has the competitor data been shared beyond the new hire?
- Does the 25% target still hold once items 2-5 are removed? If not, reset the target with the client rather than reintroducing the tactics.

**What is clear (keep):** clinical-need segmentation logic, field-force training, hospital-channel pricing review using public data.

**Records:** preserve all drafts, emails and notes relating to items 2, 5, 6 and 7.

**Note on the target:** removing the flagged tactics reduces the modelled uplift. The team should present the lower, lawful growth case and explain why, rather than leaving the client to find the gap.

*Have this reviewed by legal counsel before the steering committee.*
