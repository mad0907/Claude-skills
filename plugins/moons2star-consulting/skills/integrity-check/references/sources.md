# Sources (laws, standards and guidance)

*This toolkit references public standards, laws and widely taught concepts only. It names no companies, products or individuals. Laws below change often: verify the current text and enforcement position with qualified counsel before relying on any point.*

## 1. Bribery and corruption
- **India: Prevention of Corruption Act 1988, as amended in 2018.** Giving or promising an undue advantage is an offence (Section 8). **Section 9** makes a commercial organisation liable when a person associated with it bribes a public servant to obtain or retain business; the defence is "adequate procedures" in line with guidelines to be prescribed. *Verify:* as of late 2026, commentators report the central government has **not yet notified** those guidelines, so organisations rely on their own programmes, often modelled on the UK Bribery Act principles. Section 10 extends liability to directors and officers who consented or connived.
- **United States: Foreign Corrupt Practices Act 1977 (FCPA).** Anti-bribery provisions (payments to foreign officials to obtain or retain business) and accounting provisions (accurate books and records, adequate internal controls) for issuers. *Verify:* enforcement was paused by executive order in February 2025 and resumed under narrower DOJ guidelines issued in June 2025 (priorities include cartels and transnational criminal organisations, harm to US competitiveness, national security and serious misconduct). The statute itself did not change; the limitation period means past conduct can still be pursued, and DOJ corporate enforcement and self-disclosure policies were revised in 2026.
- **United Kingdom: Bribery Act 2010.** Offences of bribing, being bribed and bribing a foreign official; **Section 7** corporate offence of failing to prevent bribery by an associated person, with an "adequate procedures" defence. Ministry of Justice guidance sets six principles: proportionate procedures, top-level commitment, risk assessment, due diligence, communication and training, monitoring and review.
- **International:** OECD Anti-Bribery Convention (1997); UN Convention against Corruption (UNCAC; ratified by India in 2011).
- **Standards:** ISO 37001 (anti-bribery management systems); ISO 37301 (compliance management systems).

## 2. Fraud (corporate failure to prevent)
- **United Kingdom: Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023.** The **failure to prevent fraud** offence came into force on **1 September 2025** for "large organisations" (at least two of: turnover over £36m, assets over £18m, more than 250 employees; group-wide test). Liability arises when an associate commits a specified fraud intending to benefit the organisation; the defence is reasonable fraud prevention procedures; penalty is an unlimited fine.

## 3. Money laundering and sanctions
- **India: Prevention of Money-laundering Act 2002 (PMLA)** and its rules; obligations for reporting entities; Financial Intelligence Unit-India (FIU-IND).
- **Global standard:** Financial Action Task Force (FATF) Recommendations, including customer due diligence, beneficial ownership and politically exposed persons.
- **Sanctions and export controls:** UN Security Council sanctions; US OFAC and EU / UK sanctions regimes; India's SCOMET list for dual-use exports. Check the current lists before any transaction.

## 4. Tax
- **India: Income-tax Act 2025,** in force from **1 April 2026,** replacing the Income-tax Act 1961 (earlier years remain under the 1961 Act). Both include **General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR),** which allow arrangements lacking commercial substance to be disregarded. *Verify* section numbers in the new Act.
- **India:** Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income and Assets) and Imposition of Tax Act 2015; Prohibition of Benami Property Transactions Act 1988 (amended 2016).
- **International:** OECD/G20 Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) actions, including mandatory disclosure rules for aggressive tax planning in several jurisdictions.
- **Professional standards:** codes of ethics for accountants and tax practitioners (for example, the ICAI Code of Ethics and the IESBA Code) on tax planning and promoting schemes.

## 5. Medicines and medical devices
- **India: Uniform Code for Pharmaceutical Marketing Practices 2024 (UCPMP 2024),** Department of Pharmaceuticals: claims must be balanced and supported by approved product information; restrictions on gifts, travel and hospitality to healthcare professionals; rules for continuing medical education.
- **India:** Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940 and rules; Medical Devices Rules 2017; Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act 1954; National Medical Commission rules on professional conduct of doctors (gifts, travel, hospitality).
- **United States:** Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (misbranding, including promotion for unapproved uses); Anti-Kickback Statute; False Claims Act.
- **Concept:** "off-label" use is a prescriber's clinical decision; **promoting** a product for an unapproved use is the regulated act.

## 6. Competition
- **India: Competition Act 2002:** anti-competitive agreements, including cartels and bid-rigging (Section 3), and abuse of dominance (Section 4). Hub-and-spoke conduct, where a third party such as a consultant or distributor passes competitively sensitive information between competitors, can amount to a cartel.
- Comparable rules: US Sherman Act; EU Articles 101 and 102 TFEU; UK Competition Act 1998.

## 7. Insider and confidential information
- **India: SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations 2015:** unpublished price-sensitive information (UPSI); consultants and advisers can be "connected persons"; structured digital database and trading-window obligations.
- Breach of confidence and contractual confidentiality; Official Secrets Act 1923 for government information.
- **India: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023** and DPDP Rules 2025 for personal data.

## 8. Consumers and vulnerable groups
- **India: Consumer Protection Act 2019;** Central Consumer Protection Authority guidelines on misleading advertisements (2022) and dark patterns (2023).
- **Financial services:** RBI fair practices codes for lenders and recovery agents; SEBI and IRDAI conduct rules on mis-selling.

## 9. Conflicts of interest and whistleblowing
- **India: Companies Act 2013:** related-party transactions (Section 188); vigil mechanism (Section 177(9)); auditor independence (Section 144).
- **SEBI LODR Regulations 2015:** vigil mechanism (Regulation 22) and related-party rules.
- **Standard:** ISO 37002 (whistleblowing management systems).

## 10. Records and obstruction
- **India: Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023:** causing disappearance of evidence (Section 238, formerly IPC Section 201).
- **United States:** obstruction of justice and destruction of records in federal investigations (18 U.S.C. §§1512, 1519).
- **Practice:** legal holds; preserve documents once litigation or investigation is reasonably anticipated; route sensitive reviews through counsel to preserve privilege.

## Concepts used
- Red flags for third parties and intermediaries (OECD, ISO 37001 and enforcement guidance).
- "Substance over form" in tax and accounting.
- The newspaper test and stakeholder-harm analysis in business ethics.
