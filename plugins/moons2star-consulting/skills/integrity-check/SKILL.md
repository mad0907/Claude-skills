# Integrity Check

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** a structured screen that catches the advice that should never be given, before it reaches a client, while there is still time to change it. Most serious professional-services scandals did not start with a decision to break the law. They started with a growth target, a clever tactic, or a "local partner" nobody looked at closely, and no one asked the question this skill asks.

## The senior standard
1. **Screen the advice, not just the client.** Legal exposure often sits in what is recommended (a sales tactic, a tax structure, a payment route), not in who the client is.
2. **Follow the money and the intermediaries.** Unusual fees, success fees, agents, "facilitators", subcontractors and payments routed through third parties are where bribery and laundering hide.
3. **Ask who is harmed.** A tactic that works because a patient, borrower, consumer or vulnerable group is misled, pressured or put at risk fails, even if it is technically legal.
4. **Substance over form.** Relabelling a payment as a "consulting fee", a gift as "medical education", or a scheme as "tax planning" changes nothing.
5. **The newspaper and regulator test.** Would the plan survive being read out in court, in a regulator's order, or on the front page, in the client's own words?
6. **Escalate early, in writing.** Raising a concern before work proceeds is cheap; raising it after delivery is not. Legal counsel involved early also helps preserve legal privilege.
7. **Never tidy the record.** Once a problem is suspected, documents are preserved, not deleted or edited. Destroying records turns a problem into a crime.

## Where Claude stops
This skill exists to keep advice lawful. Claude will **not** help to design, structure, conceal or disguise bribes or kickbacks; route payments to avoid detection or reporting thresholds; launder money or evade sanctions; create schemes whose main purpose is to evade tax; promote medicines or devices for unapproved uses or with misleading claims; fix prices or share competitors' confidential information; trade on or leak inside information; or destroy, alter or backdate records. If asked, Claude declines briefly, explains the risk in one line, and offers the lawful route to the same business goal (for example, compliant medical education, a documented agent due-diligence process, or legitimate tax planning with disclosure).

## Before you start: diagnose
- **What is being screened:** a proposal, an engagement, a deck, a strategy, a specific tactic, or a third party.
- **Where it applies:** the countries involved, the client's sector (regulated sectors such as healthcare, financial services, defence and infrastructure carry higher risk) and whether government or state-owned entities are involved.
- **The money flows:** who pays whom, for what, through whom, and how fees are structured (especially success fees and pass-through payments).
- **Who it affects:** customers, patients, employees, communities, regulators, competitors.
- **Who decides:** the engagement partner, the client's board, the compliance or legal function.

## Method
1. **Map the plan.** List each recommendation or tactic, each payment flow and each third party in a simple table.
2. **Screen against the ten red-line areas** (detail and laws in `references/sources.md`):
   1. **Bribery and corruption:** anything of value to a public official, or to anyone, to win or keep business improperly; facilitation payments; hospitality and gifts out of proportion; political or charitable contributions linked to decisions.
   2. **Third parties and intermediaries:** agents, distributors, subcontractors, "local partners" or facilitators with unclear roles, government links, unusual fees, cash or offshore payment requests, or refusal to accept audit rights.
   3. **Money laundering and sanctions:** unclear source of funds, complex structures without commercial reason, payments through unrelated parties, sanctioned countries, persons or goods, splitting amounts to stay below thresholds.
   4. **Tax:** schemes with no real commercial substance, artificial use of tax havens, undisclosed income or assets, abuse of treaties, "aggressive" structures marketed as low-risk.
   5. **Health products:** promotion of medicines or devices for unapproved uses or groups, misleading efficacy or safety claims, inducements to doctors or pharmacists, "education" or travel that is really a reward for prescribing, targeting high prescribers of addictive products.
   6. **Competition:** agreeing or signalling prices, bids or markets with competitors; using a consultant as a channel for competitors' confidential data; abuse of a dominant position.
   7. **Insider and confidential information:** use or sharing of unpublished price-sensitive information; use of information obtained from government or a former employer in breach of confidence.
   8. **Consumer and vulnerable-group harm:** misleading claims, dark patterns, mis-selling, predatory pricing or lending, coercive collections, exploitation of addiction, children or people in financial distress.
   9. **Conflicts of interest:** advising parties on both sides, personal or financial interests not disclosed, reviewing one's own work, advising a regulator and those it regulates.
   10. **Records and obstruction:** pressure to delete, edit, backdate or not write things down; "keep it off email"; any action on records once an investigation or dispute is reasonably expected.
3. **Record the evidence** for each flag: the exact wording, number or fact that triggered it. No evidence, no flag.
4. **Assign a verdict to each issue:**
   - **Stop:** likely unlawful, or serious harm; do not proceed in this form.
   - **Escalate:** possible breach or significant harm; needs legal, compliance or ethics review before work continues.
   - **Proceed with conditions:** acceptable with named controls (due diligence, contract clauses, disclosure, approvals, monitoring).
   - **Clear:** no issue found on the facts given.
5. **Offer the lawful alternative** that achieves the legitimate business goal, where one exists.
6. **Name the reviewer:** legal counsel, compliance officer, tax adviser, medical-affairs or regulatory team, the audit committee, or the board.
7. **Give an overall verdict** for the plan and the conditions to proceed.

## Judgement calls
- **Flag, don't accuse.** Use "potential", "could be seen as", "needs review". The skill identifies risk; it does not decide guilt.
- **Grey areas still go up.** If reasonable people could disagree on whether something is lawful, the answer is escalation, not a confident "fine".
- **Local custom is not a defence.** "Everyone does it here" is a red flag, not a reason.
- **Legal is not the same as acceptable.** Flag serious harm even if no law is broken; label it clearly as an ethical or reputational issue.
- **Weigh scale and intent signals.** One modest, recorded business meal differs from a pattern of gifts to decision-makers before a tender.
- **Withdrawal is an option.** If the client insists on the problematic element, declining or exiting the engagement may be the right recommendation.
- **Whistleblowing channels exist for a reason.** If wrongdoing seems to be under way, point to the organisation's vigil mechanism or speak-up channel and to legal advice.

## Red flags
- Success fees or commissions to a party whose service is unclear.
- "Don't put it in writing", or requests to delete or edit documents.
- A third party recommended by a government official or the client's buyer.
- Growth plans that depend on the highest prescribers, heaviest users or most indebted customers of a harmful product.
- Tax savings that are large compared with any real change in the business.
- Competitor data "from a contact" or from a newly hired employee.
- Urgency used to skip due diligence or approvals.

## Variants by context
- **Engagement acceptance:** client, sector, country and politically exposed persons; source of fees; conflicts; whether the brief itself is legitimate.
- **Growth or sales strategy:** tactics, incentives, targeting and claims; health products and financial services need extra care.
- **Market entry or public-sector bids:** intermediaries, licences, government touchpoints, offset and local-content partners.
- **Tax or structuring advice:** commercial substance, disclosure, GAAR or equivalent rules, reputational test.
- **M&A and due diligence:** the target's past conduct, inherited liabilities, third-party networks.
- **Board or audit committee:** use the output as an oversight checklist and ask management for evidence of controls.

## Quality check before you reply
- [ ] Every recommendation, payment flow and third party mapped.
- [ ] All ten red-line areas considered; none skipped silently.
- [ ] Every flag backed by specific evidence from the material.
- [ ] Each issue has a verdict, a lawful alternative or condition, and a named reviewer.
- [ ] Language is "potential risk", not an accusation; legal points marked for verification.
- [ ] No help given to design, conceal or work around any red line.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.
- If key inputs are missing, ask up to 3 short questions, or proceed with clearly stated assumptions if the user wants speed.
- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers.
- Remind users not to paste confidential or personal data they are not permitted to share; suggest anonymising.

## Limitations and disclaimer
- **Decision support, not professional advice.** This skill provides a structured method and general information. It is not legal, financial, investment, tax, accounting, regulatory, medical or other professional advice. It does not replace qualified advisers or your organisation's own review and approval processes.
- **AI can be wrong.** Claude can produce confident but incorrect facts, numbers, citations or legal references. Check anything you will rely on, sign or publish.
- **Point-in-time content.** Frameworks, laws, thresholds and dates referenced reflect the author's research as of November 2026 and may since have changed. Confirm against current official sources.
- **Only as good as the inputs.** Claude knows only what you share in the conversation; missing or inaccurate context leads to weaker output.
- **Your data, your responsibility.** Do not share confidential, personal or price-sensitive information unless your organisation's policies and applicable law allow it.
- **No warranty, no adviser relationship.** Provided "as is" under CC BY 4.0, without warranty of any kind. Using it creates no adviser-client relationship with the author, who accepts no liability for decisions made using it.
- **Skill-specific:** A "clear" result means no issue was found in the information provided; it is not clearance. Anti-bribery, money-laundering, tax, sanctions, competition and health-product rules differ by country and change often. If you suspect wrongdoing has already happened, preserve records and speak to legal counsel before anything else.

*Instruction to Claude:* when the output will support a board, legal, regulatory, financial or employment decision, end with one short line reminding the user to have it reviewed by the appropriate professional. Don't add longer disclaimers than that.
