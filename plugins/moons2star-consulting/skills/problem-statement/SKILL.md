# Problem Statement

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** a one-page problem definition a sponsor can approve in two minutes. It focuses all later work on a decision and stops the project drifting into "everything about customers".

## The senior standard
1. **A problem worth solving is a decision someone must take.** No decision-maker and no deadline means a topic, not a problem.
2. **Outcome, not activity.** Frame the result in measurable business terms (revenue, cost, risk, customer, time), not in deliverables ("a report on…").
3. **Symptoms vs root problem.** "Churn rose" is a symptom. The problem might be onboarding, price, product gaps or service. Frame the question so the cause can be found, not presumed.
4. **Solution-neutral, unless the solution is already decided.** "How do we build an app to…" presupposes the answer. If the sponsor has decided, say so and reframe the question as "how to make it succeed".
5. **Scope is a promise.** Explicit out-of-scope and parked items protect delivery and relationships.
6. **The right altitude.** Big enough to matter, small enough to answer in the time available.

## Before you start: diagnose
- Who asked, in their own words, and why now (the trigger)?
- Who decides, by when, and what will they do with the answer?
- What has been tried before, and what happened?
- What constraints are fixed (budget, policy, people, technology, regulation)?
- What data or evidence exists today?

## Method
1. **Capture the ask verbatim,** plus the trigger and the history.
2. **Ask "so that…?" repeatedly** until you reach a business outcome. Ask "why does this matter?" until you reach money, risk, customers or mission.
3. **Separate symptoms from potential root causes;** list both. Keep the question open to causes.
4. **Write the question:** "What must [decision-maker] do to achieve [measurable outcome] by [date], given [constraints]?"
5. **Success criteria:** 2-4 measurable tests of a solved problem. Include a guardrail (what must not get worse).
6. **Scope:** in, out, and parked (related but later); include geographies, segments, processes and time period.
7. **Stakeholders:** who must accept the answer and what each needs to believe; known positions.
8. **Unknowns and hypotheses:** the 3-5 biggest unknowns; initial hypotheses to test (feeds the issue or hypothesis tree).
9. **Stress test:**
   - **Too broad?** Can it be answered in the time? If not, narrow it.
   - **Too narrow?** Does it presuppose a cause or solution?
   - **Solution in disguise?** Does it contain "implement", "build" or "launch"?
   - **Measurable?**
   - **Owned?**
10. **Confirm** with the sponsor and record the agreed version.

## Judgement calls
- **When the sponsor insists on a solution,** don't fight it. Frame a two-part question: will the chosen solution solve the underlying problem, and how should it be executed? Make the underlying problem explicit.
- **Political subtext** matters. The stated question may hide the real one, such as justifying a reorganisation or a budget. Ask privately what decision will actually be made.
- **Multiple decision-makers** need one accountable owner; others are stakeholders.
- **Short timelines need narrower questions.** A 4-week project can't answer a 5-year strategy question well.

## Red flags
- No named decision-maker or date.
- An outcome described as a deliverable ("a report", "a dashboard").
- A question that names the solution.
- No out-of-scope list.
- Success criteria that are adjectives ("better", "improved").

## Variants by context
- **Board ask:** frame around the board's decision and its oversight duties; include risk appetite and regulatory context.
- **Transformation:** a problem statement per workstream, plus an overarching one; link to the business case.
- **AI initiative:** frame the business problem first; AI is a candidate means, not the goal.
- **Crisis:** narrow, time-boxed question ("What must we decide in the next 48 hours to…?").
- **Public sector:** include citizen outcomes, equity, and statutory duties.

- [ ] Success criteria are measurable, with a guardrail.
- [ ] In, out and parked scope; constraints; stakeholders.
- [ ] Fits the time and resources available.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.
- If key inputs are missing, ask up to 3 short questions, or proceed with clearly stated assumptions if the user wants speed.
- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers.
- Remind users not to paste confidential or personal data they are not permitted to share; suggest anonymising.
