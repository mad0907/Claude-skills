# Worked example (illustrative; CEO of a mid-sized hospital chain meeting the state health secretary and team, 45 minutes)

**Outcome wanted:** agreement in principle to a public-private partnership pilot. The chain would run critical-care units in 3 district hospitals, with a joint working group set up within 30 days.

**Minimum acceptable:** the department nominates an officer to co-develop a proposal.

**Context:**
- District hospitals have critical-care bed shortages and staff gaps.
- The state budget announced a PPP intent for specialty care.
- Our chain runs 2 similar units in another state with outcome data.
- A previous PPP in the state faced criticism over pricing for patients.

| Attendee | Interests | Likely stance | Influence | Needs to hear |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Health Secretary | Visible improvement in outcomes; avoiding controversy; budget limits | Cautiously open | Decision-maker | Outcome evidence; patient-affordability safeguards; low fiscal risk |
| Mission Director, health programme | Programme targets; staff deployment | Neutral | High (implementation) | How it fits existing schemes; staffing model |
| Finance officer | Fiscal exposure; payment terms | Sceptical | Veto on cost | Cost per bed-day vs current; payment linked to outcomes |
| District hospital superintendent | Workload; control of the hospital | Wary | Medium | A clear governance role; no displacement of existing staff |

**Three messages:**
1. Outcomes are proven. In our two partner units, critical-care mortality fell from 21% to 15% in 2 years (independently audited).
2. Patients pay nothing. Care is free at the point of use under the state's insurance scheme, with rates at or below the scheme tariff.
3. The risk is low and the exit clear. A 3-unit pilot, outcome-linked payments, quarterly public reporting, and a step-in right for the state.

**Likely questions and answers:**

| Question | Answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| "Won't patients be charged extra?" | No. All services at these units are under the state scheme; there is no extra billing, and that is enforced by contract with penalties. | Draft contract clause; partner-state audit |
| "What happens to our staff?" | Existing staff remain government employees; we add specialists and train local staff. 40% of our partner-unit nurses were trained from the local workforce. | Staffing plan |
| "Why should the state pay more per bed-day?" | Cost per bed-day is ~8% higher, but cost per survivor is lower and length of stay shorter. Payments are linked to outcome targets. | Cost-outcome analysis |
| Hope-they-don't-ask: "Your other-state contract had a dispute" | Yes, over delayed payments in year 1. It was resolved via the contract's dispute mechanism; we've since built in escrow and monthly reconciliations. | Resolution summary |

**Asks:** a joint working group; data access for the 3 districts; nomination of a nodal officer.

**Offers:** a no-cost feasibility study; training for 50 district nurses.

**Red lines:** no open-ended unfunded mandates; payment-security mechanism required.

**Landmines:**
- Don't criticise the current district staff.
- Avoid comparisons with the previously criticised PPP by name.
- No commitments on the tender process (procurement rules apply).

**Facts at hand:** district critical-care beds (current vs norm); occupancy; our outcome data; cost per bed-day; the pilot budget.

**Agenda:** 5 min context; 15 min outcomes and model; 15 min concerns and Q&A; 10 min next steps and the ask.

**Pre-wire:** brief the Mission Director's office on the staffing model, and share the cost analysis with the finance officer a week before.

**Follow-up:** a note within 24 hours summarising agreements, owners and dates; the draft working-group terms of reference.
