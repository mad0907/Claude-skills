# Meeting Brief

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** you walk in knowing what you want, who wants what, what you'll say, what you'll be asked and how you'll answer, so the meeting produces a decision or commitment rather than a pleasant conversation.

## The senior standard
1. **Outcome first.** Define the specific outcome (a decision, commitment, information, or relationship step) and the minimum acceptable result. If you can't name it, you're not ready.
2. **Know the room.** For each attendee: role, interests (what they really care about), position on this issue, influence, history, and what they need from you.
3. **Three messages, not ten.** Leaders remember about three points. Choose them for this audience.
4. **Pre-empt the hard questions.** List the 5-8 most likely and most dangerous questions; prepare short, honest answers with evidence.
5. **Asks and offers are explicit.** What you will ask for, what you can give, and what you cannot.
6. **Landmines mapped:** sensitive history, legal or regulatory constraints, confidential topics, personal dynamics.
7. **Pre-wire.** Big decisions are rarely made in the meeting. Plan the conversations beforehand.

## Before you start: diagnose
- The meeting's purpose, date, length and format; who called it; the attendees.
- Background: history, prior commitments, open issues, documents shared.
- Your objectives and constraints (mandate, authority, what's not negotiable).
- What's at stake for each side.

## Method
1. **Outcome:** the ideal result and the minimum acceptable result; how you'll know you got it.
2. **Attendee map:** name and role | interests | likely position (supportive / neutral / opposed) | influence | what they need to hear | relationship notes.
3. **Context in 5 lines:** why now, what's happened, what's changed.
4. **Three key messages,** each with one proof point.
5. **Questions and objections:** question → a short answer (2-3 sentences) → evidence → bridge back to the messages. Include the "question you hope they don't ask".
6. **Asks and offers:** what you'll request; concessions you can make; red lines.
7. **Landmines:** topics to avoid or handle carefully; legal or disclosure constraints (e.g. price-sensitive information, ongoing litigation, regulatory matters).
8. **Facts at hand:** 5-10 numbers and dates you must know without notes.
9. **Agenda and time plan:** a suggested flow; where to spend time; when to make the ask.
10. **Pre-wiring plan:** whom to brief before, and on what.
11. **Close and follow-up:** how to summarise agreements; owners and dates; a follow-up note within 24 hours.

## Judgement calls
- **When the outcome is unrealistic for one meeting,** set a "next step" outcome (agreement to a working session, data access, a second meeting).
- **Listen more in first meetings;** state less. Diagnose before prescribing.
- **With regulators:** factual, complete, no spin; record commitments carefully; legal counsel where appropriate.
- **With boards:** lead with the decision needed and the key risks; respect the governance process.
- **Bad news:** say it early, own it, bring a plan.

## Red flags
- No defined outcome.
- Unknown attendees' interests.
- Ten messages; no answers to obvious hard questions.
- Asks not stated; no red lines.
- No follow-up plan.

## Variants by context
- **Board or committee:** the decision, the options, the risks; the pre-read is concise; questions anticipated from each director's expertise.
- **Investor:** strategy, performance, guidance consistency; disclosure-rule awareness (no selective price-sensitive information).
- **Regulator:** facts, compliance status, remediation; legal review; a record of the meeting.
- **Customer or partner:** their goals, value delivered, issues, the expansion or renewal ask.
- **Negotiation:** combine with `negotiation-prep`.
- **First meeting with a new stakeholder:** relationship and listening; learn their priorities.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.
- If key inputs are missing, ask up to 3 short questions, or proceed with clearly stated assumptions if the user wants speed.
- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers.
- Remind users not to paste confidential or personal data they are not permitted to share; suggest anonymising.
