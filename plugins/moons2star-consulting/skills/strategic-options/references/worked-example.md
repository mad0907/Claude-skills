# Worked example (illustrative; fictional Indian engineering group with an underperforming European subsidiary)

**Decision:** future of the German subsidiary (revenue €95M; EBITDA −€4M; 420 staff; holds the group's only EU manufacturing licence for rail components). Decide by the March board.
**Red lines:** no breach of EU customer supply contracts (penalties); works-council consultation is mandatory for restructuring.

**Options:**
- **A.** Turnaround in place (cost programme + product-mix shift).
- **B.** Merge operations into the UK subsidiary, keep a German sales and service office, and transfer the licence (if transferable; legal view needed).
- **C.** Sell to a strategic buyer, with a supply agreement.
- **D.** Close and exit the EU rail market.
- **Status quo:** cost of inaction ≈ −€4-6M EBITDA a year and rising, plus €12M of equipment refresh due in 2028.

## Criteria (agreed with the CEO, CFO and Board Chair on 10 Jan)
Financial value 30%; EU market access 20%; risk 15%; capital required 10%; time to impact 10%; people and reputation 10%; reversibility 5%.

| Criterion (weight) | A Turnaround | B Merge with UK | C Sell | D Close | Status quo |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Financial value (30%) | NPV +€8M (if the plan delivers) → 3 | NPV +€15M → 4 | Proceeds €25-40M + supply margin → 4 | −€30M (closure costs, lost margin) → 1 | −€25M → 1 |
| EU market access (20%) | Full → 5 | Depends on licence transfer → 3 | Via supply agreement → 3 | Lost → 1 | Full → 5 |
| Risk (15%) | Execution risk high → 2 | Licence and integration risk → 3 | Buyer and pricing risk → 3 | Reputational, contract penalties → 1 | Continuing losses → 1 |
| Capital (10%) | €14M → 2 | €12M → 3 | Nil → 5 | €18M (social plan, penalties) → 1 | €12M refresh → 2 |
| Time to impact (10%) | 24-30 months → 2 | 18 months → 3 | 9-12 months → 4 | 12 months → 3 | — → 1 |
| People and reputation (10%) | Positive → 4 | ~150 roles affected → 2 | Jobs largely preserved → 4 | 420 roles → 1 | — → 3 |
| Reversibility (5%) | High → 4 | Medium → 3 | Low → 1 | Very low → 1 | High → 5 |
| **Weighted** | **3.20** | **3.20** | **3.60** | **1.20** | **2.30** |

**Sensitivity:**
- If EU market access is weighted 30% (cutting financial value to 20%), the gap narrows to A 3.40 vs C 3.50.
- **If the licence is not transferable, B drops to 2.80.**
- The ranking is **close between A and C.** It hinges on (1) belief in turnaround execution, and (2) how strategic direct EU access is.

**Trade-off:** selling (C) crystallises €25-40M and removes execution risk but gives up direct control of EU access; a turnaround (A) keeps access but needs €14M and 2+ years of management attention with high execution risk.

**Recommendation:** run a **dual track for 6 months**:
- Launch a buyer sounding (C).
- In parallel, implement the no-regret turnaround steps from A (pricing, procurement, footprint study).
- Decide in September based on the bids received vs turnaround progress.

**Conditions:** an independent legal opinion on licence transfer; works-council consultation plan; customer contract review.

**Reversal triggers:**
- Bids below €20M → favour A.
- Turnaround milestones missed by >20% at month 6 → favour C.

**Review date:** board meeting, September.

**Decision record:** dissent from the COO, who favours B. The record notes this to be revisited if the licence is confirmed transferable.
