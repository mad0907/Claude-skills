# Competitor Benchmark

*moons2star Consultant's Toolkit · created by Dr. Munmun Das · [moons2star.in](https://moons2star.in)*

**What this gives you:** a fair, evidence-based picture of where you stand against the alternatives customers actually consider, and what competitors are likely to do next, translated into specific implications.

## The senior standard
1. **The competitive set is defined by customers,** not by industry codes. Include substitutes and new business models (including AI-native entrants).
2. **Criteria come from choice drivers.** What do customers choose on (price, quality, speed, service, breadth, trust, integration)? Pair them with economics (growth, margin, cost ratios) and capabilities.
3. **Like for like.** Normalise definitions, fiscal years, currencies and segments. Label estimates.
4. **Look forward.** Strategy, investments, hiring, partnerships and pricing moves signal future positions.
5. **So what.** End with implications: where to defend, attack, differentiate, or avoid.
6. **Ethical intelligence only.** Public sources, customer and expert interviews conducted properly, and your own data. No confidential information obtained improperly; comply with competition law (no sharing of competitively sensitive information).

## Before you start: diagnose
- The decision (pricing, product, entry, investment) and the segment or geography.
- The customer choice drivers (from research, win/loss analysis, reviews).
- Available data: filings, investor materials, websites, reviews, mystery shopping, expert calls, win/loss data.

## Method
1. **Define the set:** 4-8 competitors across three rings (direct, adjacent, substitutes or new entrants).
2. **Select criteria (8-12):** customer-facing (price, quality, features, service levels, speed, coverage, brand trust), economic (revenue growth, margins, cost ratios, scale), and capability (technology, data, talent, distribution, partnerships).
3. **Collect data:** cite sources and dates; mark estimates; normalise.
4. **Benchmark table:** us vs each competitor; highlight gaps (lead / parity / lag).
5. **Positioning map:** pick the two dimensions customers value most; plot competitors; identify clusters and white space.
6. **Competitor profiles:** strategy, recent moves, strengths, vulnerabilities, likely next moves and triggers.
7. **Win/loss insight** (if data exists): why we win and lose against each.
8. **Implications:** where we lead and must defend; where we lag and must fix or avoid; white space to attack; competitor moves to pre-empt.

## Judgement calls
- **Benchmarks against the best competitor in each dimension** (not one average) reveal achievable targets.
- **Small, fast entrants** often matter more than large incumbents. Track their customer traction, not their size.
- **Beware vanity metrics** (feature counts). Weight criteria by customer importance.
- **Public filings lag.** Combine them with timely signals (job postings, product releases, pricing pages, customer reviews).

## Red flags
- Only the familiar rival is analysed.
- Mixed periods or definitions.
- Feature checklists with no customer weighting.
- No implications.
- Information from questionable sources (former employees sharing confidential data).

## Variants by context
- **Pricing decisions:** price ladders, discount practices, value-for-money positioning.
- **Investment / M&A:** financial benchmarks (growth, margins, returns, valuation multiples).
- **Banking and insurance:** cost-to-income, NIM or combined ratio, digital adoption, NPS, regulatory ratios.
- **Technology / SaaS:** product capability, integrations, implementation time, net revenue retention, pricing model.
- **Operations:** cost per unit, productivity, quality, delivery performance.

## Quality check before you reply
- [ ] The competitive set includes substitutes and new entrants.
- [ ] Criteria are weighted by customer choice drivers, plus economics and capabilities.
- [ ] Normalised data with sources and dates; estimates labelled.
- [ ] A positioning map and forward-looking profiles.
- [ ] Clear implications; ethical and competition-law-compliant sourcing.

## House rules
- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources. Label estimates "(est.)" and list assumptions openly.
- If key inputs are missing, ask up to 3 short questions, or proceed with clearly stated assumptions if the user wants speed.
- Answer first, then support. Plain English; define any jargon.
- Use the user's currency, units, terminology and context (e.g. ₹ and lakh/crore for Indian users).
- Do calculations with code or a spreadsheet when available, not mental arithmetic.
- For legal, regulatory, tax or accounting specifics, flag them for verification against current official sources and qualified advisers.
- Remind users not to paste confidential or personal data they are not permitted to share; suggest anonymising.
