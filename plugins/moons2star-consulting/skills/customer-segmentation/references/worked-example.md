# Worked example (illustrative; fictional logistics-software provider, 800 B2B customers)

**Decision:** redesign sales coverage and customer-success models for next year.
**Basis:** needs (scale and integration complexity, growth stage) + value (annual contract value, margin) + behaviour (product usage, support load).

| Segment | Customers | Revenue / margin | Growth | Needs | Behaviour | Cost-to-serve | Position | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scaling shippers | 112 (14%) | ₹48 Cr (31%) / 62% | +22% | Multi-warehouse, APIs, carrier integrations | Heavy API use; frequent change requests | High | Strong | **1: invest** |
| Stable mid-market | 260 (33%) | ₹52 Cr (34%) / 68% | +6% | Reliability, compliance reports | Steady usage; low support | Medium | Strong | 2: retain and upsell |
| Small digital-first | 330 (41%) | ₹21 Cr (14%) / 55% | +15% | Self-serve, low price | Low usage depth; churn 18% | Low (digital) | Medium | 3: automate |
| Enterprise pilots | 18 (2%) | ₹19 Cr (12%) / 40% | +40% | Custom workflows, security reviews | Long cycles; bespoke | Very high | Weak | 4: selective |
| Dormant / at-risk | 80 (10%) | ₹14 Cr (9%) / 50% | −20% | — | Usage down >50% over 6 months | Medium | — | Win-back or exit |

**Assignment rules:**
1. ACV ≥ ₹1 Cr and security review → Enterprise.
2. Else, ≥3 warehouses or API calls ≥50k/month → Scaling.
3. Else, ACV ≥ ₹10 L → Stable mid-market.
4. Else → Small digital-first.
5. Override: usage −50% over 6 months → At-risk.

Coverage: 97% assigned automatically; 3% by manual review.

**What changes:**
- **Scaling:** a named solution architect and CSM; an integration roadmap council.
- **Stable:** pooled CSMs; a compliance-reporting add-on.
- **Small:** onboarding automation; in-app help; monthly plans.
- **Enterprise:** pursue only deals with a ≥50% product fit; price the professional services fully.
- **At-risk:** a 60-day win-back playbook.

**Validation:** reviewed with 6 sales leads and 20 customers; a pilot of the new coverage model in 2 regions for Q1.
